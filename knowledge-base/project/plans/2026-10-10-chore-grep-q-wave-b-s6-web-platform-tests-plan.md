## Problem Statement

`producer | grep -q P` under `set -o pipefail` reads a successful match as failure once the producer still has unwritten data when grep exits (SIGPIPE, rc 141, or EPIPE where the runner ignores the signal); negated and `&&`-chained forms read the opposite way (a false pass). The guard asserts zero outside a deferral table. After S5, one glob row remains that owns real hits: `apps/web-platform/*.test.sh` (177 lines, 37 files, slack 3). Leaving it keeps a ceiling in which new instances can hide (slack 3, and `<=` mode) and keeps the largest reading debt of the series open.

## Proposed Solution

### The conversion form

`producer | grep -q<flags> P` becomes `producer | grep -c<flags> >/dev/null P`: the early-exit letter `q` becomes `c` and one redirect is inserted after the flag cluster. `grep -c` exits 0 iff a line was selected, exactly like `grep -q`, reads its whole input (the producer never takes SIGPIPE) and is valid in `/bin/sh`. The drain probe (AC-3) re-proves it on the dev host and in `ubuntu:24.04`. Do not rewrite it to a here-string: for stdin operands the exit status is the same and the producer is read to EOF. `-m`/`--max-count` forms (output-bearing, still an early exit) become `grep -m1 P < <(producer)`.
