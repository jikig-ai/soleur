# Terms & Conditions contradiction register

**This register exists because a row that looked like it tracked these did not.**
`compliance-posture.md` carried a row reading *"T&C blanket statement contradictions | #736 | OPEN"*
for months. #736 is CLOSED and its actual subject is *"legal: update Terms & Conditions for web
platform cloud services"* — a different matter entirely. The contradictions it appeared to track
were therefore **untracked**, and no prior enumeration existed anywhere in the corpus (confirmed by
`git log` on the canonical and a repo-wide grep). This document is the durable record that row
falsely implied.

Each entry quotes **both sides by content anchor**, not line number, so the citation survives edits
to the documents it cites.

## How to read the verdicts

`REAL` items were resolved in #7349. `NOT REAL` items are recorded **with their measurement** so a
future reader does not re-derive them — and, in two cases, so that a *wrong* clearing is not
inherited a second time. Two entries below reverse an earlier ruling; that is deliberate and the
reversal is stated rather than absorbed.

## Contradictions

### E1 — Liability cap: two different figures

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §11.2 "Aggregate Liability Cap" | *"SHALL NOT EXCEED THE GREATER OF (A) THE AMOUNT YOU PAID US (IF ANY) IN THE TWELVE (12) MONTHS PRECEDING THE CLAIM, OR (B) ONE HUNDRED EUROS (EUR 100)."* |
| `disclaimer.md` §3.2 "Aggregate Liability Cap" (before) | *"shall not exceed the amount you paid to us for the Platform in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim"* — no EUR 100 floor |

**Verdict: REAL.** **Resolution:** the T&C governs — it is the instrument with an acceptance
record; the Disclaimer has none. `disclaimer.md` §3.2 now states the greater-of formula and
cross-references T&C §11.2 as prevailing.

**Corroboration the first draft missed:** `data-processing-agreement-template.md` §12.1 already
pegs its cap to *"the Soleur ToS §11.2 — the greater of (a) EUR 100"*. Had E1 been fixed in the
other direction (editing the T&C down to the Disclaimer's figure), that third instrument would
have gone silently stale.

### E1b — The cap rested on a factually false premise

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| `disclaimer.md` §3.2 (before) | *"**As the Platform is provided free of charge, this amount is zero (EUR 0.00 / USD $0.00).**"* |
| T&C §1 Definitions | *"**"Subscription"** refers to a paid plan for Web Platform access, managed through Stripe Checkout."* |

**Verdict: REAL — found by the CLO, absent from the issue.** **Resolution:** the premise is false
for every paying Web Platform user. The cap now distinguishes the free self-hosted Plugin from the
paid Web Platform.

### E1c — Unqualified exclusion of direct damages

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| `disclaimer.md` §3.1 (before) | *"- **Direct damages** arising from your use of or inability to use the Platform;"* |
| T&C §11.1 | excludes indirect, incidental, special, consequential and punitive damages — **not** direct |

**Verdict: REAL — found by the CLO, absent from the issue.** **Resolution:** deleted. An
unqualified direct-damages exclusion against an EU consumer is very likely unenforceable under
Directive 93/13 Annex 1(b), and its presence risks the entire limitation clause. Direct damages
remain subject to the §3.2 cap; they are no longer excluded.

### E2 — Two exclusive forums for the same dispute

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| DPD §11.2 (before) | *"Any disputes … shall be subject to the **exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of the Member State in which the User is established**, unless otherwise required by mandatory law."* |
| T&C §15.2 "Jurisdiction" | *"Any disputes … shall be subject to the **exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of Paris, France**."* |
| `disclaimer.md` §8.2 | identical to T&C §15.2 |

**Verdict: REAL AS FILED. This reverses an earlier CLO ruling** recorded in the plan's Domain
Review, which asserted *"the DPD has no governing-law and no forum clause at all"* and directed the
implementer **not** to fix a forum conflict. That assertion was false: DPD §11 "Governing Law and
Jurisdiction" exists and carries all three paragraphs. The error was caught only because task 0.8
requires the implementer to confirm such claims rather than inherit them.

**Why the four candidate savings routes do not reach it:**

- The DPD's *"unless otherwise required by mandatory law"* is a savings clause against **statute**;
  it does not yield to another contract term.
- DPD §11.3 preserved **data-subject** rights, but the DPD's "User" is the **Controller**. A
  controller suing over a DPD undertaking is not exercising a data-subject right.
- The T&C and Disclaimer consumer carve-outs address **consumers** only, and do not say which of
  two competing exclusive clauses binds Jikigai.
- GDPR Art. 79(2) gives a **data subject** a home forum by statute — the same claimant §11.3
  already covered. It leaves the controller-vs-provider universe untouched.

**Resolution:** fixed at the source. The DPD says of itself *"this is not a Data Processing
Agreement under Article 28. It is a disclosure document"* — and a disclosure with no acceptance
record cannot found an Art. 25 forum agreement. §11 now yields to the Terms and states the
data-subject routes expressly (Art. 77, Art. 79(2), home forum), so the net effect on the reader is
**more** protective than the clause it replaces.

**T&C §15.2 was deliberately not edited.** Carving data-protection disputes out of the Paris forum
would fragment one factual dispute across two Member States — the irreconcilable-judgments problem
Brussels I recast exists to prevent.

### E3 — Plugin-local absolutes that do not exclude operator-assisted processing

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §4.2 (before) | *"These interactions occur through your own API keys and accounts."* |
| DPD §2.1c | operator-assisted processing uses *"a Jikigai machine, a Jikigai-held credential, or both"* |

**Verdict: REAL.** The plan's supporting count (*"T&C 0 scope blocks"*) was **wrong** — §4.1 and
§8.1 do carry *"This section applies to the Plugin only"* sentences. The defect survives for a
different reason: operator-assisted processing runs **the Plugin itself** on a Jikigai machine, so
a Plugin-only scope sentence **includes** that configuration rather than excluding it.

**Resolution:** three scope paragraphs added at §4.1, §4.2 and §8.1, each using the **paragraph**
referent rather than a section referent (both sections contain "Web Platform", which trips gate 1
arm (a) on a section-scoped referent). §4.2's paragraph was split so the block precedes the bullets
and its referent is exact. The pre-existing scope sentences were left untouched — rewriting them
would have made them added lines carrying a section referent.

### E4 — Processor list four long against twelve

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §8.1b (before) | Supabase, Stripe, Hetzner, Cloudflare |
| DPD §4.2 Web Platform table | those four **plus** Sentry, Resend, **Anthropic PBC**, Cloudflare R2, FreeTSA, LinkedIn Ireland, Microsoft Ireland, Bullet Train (Flagsmith) |

**Verdict: REAL.** Anthropic's absence is the serious limb — it receives **message content**, and
§8.1b purports to state what the Web Platform processes.

**Resolution:** Anthropic PBC named as recipient of conversation content with its transfer
safeguards, and the section now points at DPD §4.2 as the authoritative enumeration rather than
attempting an exhaustive list that will drift again.

### E5 — Controller of "all", yet answerable under Arts. 15–22

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §3b.1 (before) | *"The Workspace Owner is the controller … of **all** personal data processed by the Web Platform on behalf of the workspace"*; *"Soleur acts as a processor … with respect to **all such data**"* |
| T&C §3b.2 | *"Each Co-Member retains, **against Jikigai**, the independent rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and objection conferred by **Articles 15 through 22** GDPR"* |

**Verdict: REAL AS FILED.** Rights under Arts. 15–22 run against a **controller**. Both statements
cannot be true.

> **Measurement warning — a false negative that cost a round.** `grep -E "Article (1[5-9]|2[0-2])"`
> returns **no** match for this clause, because the corpus writes the **plural** *"Articles 15
> through 22"* and the pattern requires a space where "Articles" has an `s`. During implementation
> this register's author used that pattern, concluded the clause did not exist, and reported E5 as
> a phantom. Use `Articles? 1[5-9]`. Reading only the first sentence of §3b.2 (its Art. 13(1)(e)
> recipient framing) produces the same wrong answer.

**Resolution:** §3b.1 rewritten — see E5b.

### E5b — Jikigai's own accountability ledgers assigned to the Owner

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §3b.1 (before) | Owner controls *"the per-(founder, action_class) scope grants and per-send signature ledger records"* |
| `article-30-register.md` header | the register is *"scoped to Jikigai's **controller** capacity only"*, and PA-14, PA-16, PA-18 sit in it |

**Verdict: REAL — found during re-derivation; in neither the issue nor the plan.** The same applies
to PA-1 (account), PA-3 (billing), PA-11 (consent) and PA-19/PA-20 (membership audit).

**Resolution:** §3b.1 now separates three roles — Owner as controller of workspace content and
workspace-activity records; Jikigai as controller of Co-Member account data, subscription records
and its own Art. 5(2) accountability ledgers; and Jikigai as processor only where and to the extent
it processes on the Owner's documented instructions.

### E6 — Processor status asserted in the present tense with no instrument

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §3b.1 (before) | *"Soleur **acts** as a processor (Article 4(8) GDPR) for the Workspace Owner"* |
| T&C §3b.4 (same section) | *"**Once** Jikigai publishes …"* — the customer DPA does not yet exist |
| `tenant-dpa-register.md` | `status: draft-empty-template`; signed-row count **0** |
| DPD §2.1c(c) | *"Jikigai **will not** carry out operator-assisted processing without first agreeing a written Article 28(3) instrument"* |

**Verdict: REAL.** Art. 28(3) makes a processor relationship exist only under a written instrument.
The T&C contradicted the DPD *and* itself.

**Resolution:** narrowed to the instrument-first posture the DPD already states. **Deliberately not
fixed by drafting an inline Art. 28(3) instrument** — the DPA template lives in `knowledge-base/`,
is unpublished, and cannot be incorporated by reference into a contract the user can read. That is
a new instrument deserving its own PR and external-counsel attention. **Successor issue required.**

### E7 — Share links described as processor-capacity processing

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §8.1c (before) | *"Jikigai acts as a **processor**"* |
| `article-30-register.md` PA-4 | sits in the Art. 30(1) **controller** register, with viewer IP addresses under **Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest** |

**Verdict: REAL, and sharper than filed.** A processor has no lawful basis of its own; Art. 6(1)(f)
is a controller's determination. Nobody instructs the IP logging, the `noindex` directive, the
no-analytics choice, or the revocation semantics.

**Resolution:** §8.1c states controller capacity and cites PA-4.

**Downstream question this PR does not answer:** a controller designation carries obligations, and
Art. 13/14 notice to unauthenticated share-link viewers is the live one. Recorded for counsel
review.

### E8 — Three limbs, one real

**(a) "AUP cross-references a nonexistent T&C section" — NOT REAL.** Measured: every T&C section
referenced anywhere in the corpus resolves. Verify with the cross-reference sweep in
`knowledge-base/project/specs/feat-one-shot-7349-legal-corpus-defects/tasks.md` task 0.8.

**(b) "Side letter mandatory vs optional" — NOT REAL.** Two distinct instruments. AUP §5.6 mandates
the *Delegation Consent Side Letter*, expressly *"distinct from the workspace co-member Side Letter
in Section 5.5"*, which T&C §3b.4 makes optional. Both correct as written.

**(c) BYOK joint vs sole controllership — REAL.**

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| AUP §5.6 and DPD §2.3(w) | Grantor and Grantee are *"**joint controllers** within the meaning of Article 26 GDPR"* |
| T&C §3b.1 (before) | *"BYOK API-key usage"* listed inside the Owner's *"all personal data"* |

**Resolution:** §3b.1 gains a BYOK delegation carve-out stating the Art. 26 joint-controller
arrangement and cross-referencing AUP §5.6 and DPD §2.3(w).

### E9 — Fixed component counts

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §4 (before) | *"45 AI agents … across **five** domains"*, *"45 skills"* |
| Privacy Policy (before) | *"45 AI agents, 45 skills"* |
| GDPR Policy §1 (before) | record: *"45 agents, 45 skills"*; **published mirror: "60+ agents, 60+ skills"** — the two surfaces stated different counts of the same product, and both were wrong |
| Filesystem, measured 2026-08-11 | **68** agent files, **96** skill directories, **9** domains |

**Verdict: REAL.** **Resolution:** counts **removed, not restated**. A fixed count in a versioned
instrument is false the day the next agent lands and buys a version bump every release. The same
treatment was applied to the AUP and the Cookie Policy — the latter carried "45 agents" in the
record against **"60+ agents"** on the published page, a divergence the drift normaliser could not
see because its pattern matches `[0-9]+ AI agents`, not a bare `[0-9]+ agents`.

### E10 — Privacy Policy assigned beta-CRM controllership to the user

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| Privacy Policy §4.7 CRM bullet (before) | *"**you act as the data controller** for that data and Jikigai processes it on your behalf"* |
| Art. 30 register PA-30, DPD §2.3(ad), GDPR Policy §3.13, beta-CRM LIA (all as corrected in #7349) | *"As this activity runs today, **Jikigai is the controller**"* |

**Verdict: REAL — found at merge sign-off, and this PR is implicated.** #7349 corrected the
beta-CRM controllership in four artifacts and missed the one a data subject actually reads.

**Aggravating, and the reason it is recorded rather than quietly fixed:** the canonical carried
this sentence before the PR but the **mirror did not**. The Phase 3c drift-reduction port
*published it to soleur.ai*. A drift reduction is normally strictly protective — here it copied a
defect onto the published surface, and no gate can see that, because the gates measure divergence
between surfaces, not truth.

**Resolution:** both surfaces now state that Jikigai is the controller as the capability runs
today, that the role flips on the first non-Jikigai store owner under a written Art. 28(3)
instrument, and that the basis is Art. 6(1)(f) with Art. 14 notice to affected third parties.

**Lesson for the next port:** verify what a ported passage *claims*, not only that it is textually
identical to the record. A port is legally a first publication, and the record can be wrong.

## Ambiguities — recorded, not resolved

The plan asserted *"six ambiguities"* but **never enumerated them**, in this document's terms or
anywhere else in the corpus. Rather than pad this list to match that number — which would make this

### E-amb1 — Cap scope: "amount you paid us" vs "subscription fees"

| Side | Statement |
|---|---|
| T&C §11.2(A) | *"the amount you paid us (if any)"* |
| `data-processing-agreement-template.md` §12.1 | *"subscription fees … under the Web Platform subscription"* |

The T&C limb is **broader**. Not a contradiction — the DPA template's cap cannot exceed the T&C's —
but the two describe different measures and a reader could take either as the operative figure.
**Recorded, not resolved.**

### E-amb2 — Whether the DPD is inside the §16.1 bundle

T&C §16.1's parenthetical previously named only the Privacy Policy and the AUP. Whether the DPD,
Cookie Policy and Disclaimer were inside the "entire agreement" was arguable. **Partially resolved
in #7349** — §16.1 now names all five — but whether naming a *disclosure* document in an
entire-agreement clause gives it contractual force is left open deliberately.
