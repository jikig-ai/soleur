# Legitimate Interest Assessment — alpha-tester repository observation

> **DRAFT — This document was generated by AI and requires professional legal review before use. It does not constitute legal advice.**

## Scope, and why the beta-CRM LIA does not cover this

Two activities are assessed, both with **Jikigai as controller**:

- **PA-35 (primary, live and ongoing):** the operator holds GitHub collaborator access to an alpha tester's **private** repository and reads the git history of its `knowledge-base/` tree to measure knowledge-base growth for Jikigai's own validation metrics (#1442).
- **PA-34 (session-bound):** the dogfooding limb of the operator-assisted run of 2026-08-06 — observing how Soleur performs against a real third-party codebase, for Jikigai's own product learning.

`knowledge-base/legal/legitimate-interest-assessments/2026-07-07-beta-crm-lia.md` does **not** cover either. That LIA addresses the operator's notes *about* testers, where the **operator** is controller and Jikigai is processor of the operator's own capture store. It says nothing about tester-owned data that Soleur or the operator reads, transforms or writes. The roles are inverted here.

`PA-32` (community observation and republication) does not cover it either: PA-32 is scoped to **public** activity, and this repository is private.

---

## Purpose

**PA-35.** Establish whether the alpha programme is producing its intended effect, by measuring growth of the tester's `knowledge-base/` tree over time. This is one of three validation metrics for the alpha cohort; the runbook's own measurability table records that the other two — return/session frequency and non-engineering agent usage — are **not measurable on the self-hosted CLI surface regardless**. So this access buys **one metric of three**.

**PA-34.** Improve Soleur by observing its behaviour against a real third-party codebase under guided onboarding. Jikigai determines this purpose; the tester does not instruct it.

Both are genuine commercial interests. Neither is a compelling or vital interest, and neither carries any legal obligation — so Art. 6(1)(f) is the only candidate basis, and it must survive all three limbs to be available at all.

---

## Necessity

**This limb is where the assessment is most exposed, and it is recorded that way rather than argued past.**

### A less intrusive means exists, was considered, and was declined

The metric PA-35 buys — knowledge-base growth — is fully obtainable **without any repository access**: the tester can supply the output of `git log --stat`, or simply a periodic count. That alternative yields the same single metric, introduces no access to repository content, and touches no third-party personal data.

The operator elected to retain collaborator access on **measurement-fidelity** grounds — a self-supplied figure depends on the tester remembering, and is not independently verifiable — **not because the alternative was unavailable**. Decision UC-1, recorded 2026-08-06.

An LIA that omitted the rejected alternative would fail the very balancing test it claims to perform, so it is recorded here in the necessity limb where it bears, not relegated to a footnote. *C-13/16 Rīgas satiksme* and EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 both put necessity **before** balancing: where the interest can be achieved by a less intrusive means, the processing is not necessary and Art. 6(1)(f) fails without reaching the balance.

**Honest assessment: the necessity limb is weak for PA-35 as currently configured.** Convenience and fidelity are not necessity. The processing is *defensible* only on a reading where independent verifiability is itself part of the interest — that the metric must be trustworthy to be worth collecting at all. Counsel should test that reading rather than assume it.

**A cheaper de-escalation than either option, recorded because it is free:** re-derive the metric from **non-personal aggregates** — commit counts, file counts, directory growth — instead of reading repository content. That keeps the metric, keeps independent verifiability, and takes the activity out of Art. 4(1) scope almost entirely, because non-personal aggregates are not personal data. This is the recommended path and it costs nothing.

### PA-34 necessity

Narrower and stronger. Observing a real run is not substitutable by synthetic material if the purpose is learning how the product behaves in the field. But note the determination's §6 residual: the dogfooding limb and the instructed limb were served by **one indivisible set of operations**. That means the *incremental* processing attributable to the dogfooding purpose may be nil — nothing extra was read for it. That cuts both ways, and is addressed in the balance below.

## Balancing

### (i) Nature of the controller's interests

Commercial and developmental, not compelling. Jikigai is a one-person company validating a product with its first external user. The interest is real but modest, and the cohort is ten testers, not a market.

### (ii) Nature of the data and data subjects

The data subjects are **involuntary third parties**. They are not Soleur users, not the tester's counterparties in any Jikigai-facing sense, and have no relationship with Jikigai of any kind. They did not choose the tester's supplier and cannot influence it.

Categories: company officers named in RNE *pouvoirs* fixture data (names, corporate roles); git commit authorship (name, email, timestamp); any incidental personal data in committed content. No Art. 9 or Art. 10 special categories are identified — public-register officer data is not an Art. 9 category.

**Volume is low and this matters to the balance.** The reconstruction of the 2026-08-06 session found **no database records were processed**: no query-result rows, no data-bearing files opened, no seed data. The working material was schema, configuration, tests and prose. The exposure is a fixtures file surfaced in a directory listing, not a venture database.

### (iii) Reasonable expectations of the data subject

**This is the limb that goes against the processing, and it is not close.**

A company officer named in a French public register does not expect their details to be read by a **software vendor of a business that holds their record**, for that vendor's own product-improvement purposes. The register's publicity establishes an expectation of *availability*, not of onward use by arbitrary third parties for unrelated purposes. Publicity is not consent, and it does not travel to secondary purposes.

Git commit authors in a private repository have an even stronger expectation: a private repository is private, and a supplier's collaborator access is not something a contributor is typically told about.

### (iv) Impact on the data subject

**Low in severity, and that is the honest reading.** The processing is read-only, produces no decision about any data subject, no profiling, no automated decision-making within Art. 22, no onward disclosure, and no publication. Nobody's access to credit, employment or services is affected.

The residual risk is **confidentiality**: content from a private repository surfacing in a Soleur commit, issue, digest or case study. That risk is real — this repository has a recorded incident class of exactly that shape (PA-32, where 80 digests were published carrying commenter handles and stargazer names). It is addressed in safeguards below.

### (v) Safeguards

1. **Behavioural control (determination C1), effective immediately:** no further Jikigai-keyed runs against tester content until an Art. 28(3) instrument exists.
2. **No republication.** Content observed under PA-35 must not enter any Soleur commit, issue, digest, case study or marketing artifact. This is the PA-32 failure mode and it is named here so it cannot be reached by inattention.
3. Access is **repository-scoped** collaborator access, not organisation-wide, and read-only in practice.
4. **No systematic copy retained.** Only derived aggregate metrics persist.
5. **Access terminates at end of alpha**, with local clones and retained feedback artifacts deleted and written confirmation within 30 days — wired into the onboarding runbook's offboarding step.
6. **Art. 14 notice** as below.
7. **Recommended:** re-derive to non-personal aggregates, which removes most of this analysis's exposure at no cost.

### Art. 14 transparency for involuntary data subjects

The data are not obtained from the data subject, so Art. 14 applies. Direct notice to company officers named in a fixture file, or to every commit author in a tester's repository, is **not proportionate** — Jikigai has no reliable contact route to them and would have to process further personal data to construct one.

**Art. 14(5)(b) disproportionate effort is claimed, and it is claimed in writing here rather than assumed.** The claim rests on: no existing contact channel; the data subjects are numerous and unenumerated; contacting them would require obtaining more personal data than the processing itself uses; and the processing is read-only with no effect on them.

Art. 14(5)(b) requires compensating measures where the exemption is relied on — the Article names *"making the information publicly available"* specifically. Those measures here are: **(1)** this LIA, and **(2)** the PA-34/PA-35 register entries — both of which are **published**, because `jikig-ai/soleur` is a public repository, which is what satisfies the Article's publicity limb. An earlier draft listed a third measure, *"the public disclosure route below"*; **no such route existed below, and the claim is withdrawn rather than left dangling** — a compensating measure that resolves to nothing is exactly the defect this document exists to avoid. **Weakness recorded, not smoothed:** Art. 14(5)(b)'s qualifier reads *"in particular for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes"*. Commercial product-improvement and validation metrics are none of those. That is the first point counsel or a regulator will raise, and it is recorded here in the same register as the necessity limb's weakness rather than argued around.

**Adjacency flagged, not buried:** this is the same Art.-14-to-unreachable-third-parties shape as **#7120**, whose notice clock **expired**. A second instance of the same shape must not be created silently. If counsel concludes the exemption does not hold here, it very likely does not hold there either, and both should move together.

### Art. 17 path

Erasure requests can be honoured for PA-35 by revoking access and deleting local clones — the processing holds no systematic copy, so there is little to erase. For PA-34, content that egressed to Anthropic under the Jikigai key lapses with the **30-day retention window** (approximately 2026-09-05 for the 2026-08-06 session); Jikigai cannot accelerate deletion inside Anthropic's window while `zero_retention_amendment` is `unsigned`.

### Conclusion

**PA-34: Art. 6(1)(f) is available.** The incremental processing is nil, the impact is low, and the interest is genuine. Subject to the determination's §6 residual on purpose-severability.

**PA-35: Art. 6(1)(f) is available only marginally, and the assessment does not present it as comfortable.** Necessity is weak (a less intrusive means exists and was declined) and reasonable expectations run against it. What carries it is the low severity of impact and the safeguards above — not the strength of the interest.

**The recommendation this LIA makes is to change the processing rather than to rely on this balance.** Re-deriving the metric to non-personal aggregates removes the activity from Art. 4(1) scope almost entirely, preserves the metric and its verifiability, and costs nothing. Retaining content-reading access is a defensible choice, but it is the weaker of two available positions, and this document should not be cited as though it concluded otherwise.
