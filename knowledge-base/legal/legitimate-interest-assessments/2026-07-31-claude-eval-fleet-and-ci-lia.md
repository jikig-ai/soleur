# Legitimate Interest Assessment — Anthropic-egressing fleet, community republication, and the CI surface (#7100)

This LIA is the Art. 6(1)(f) record for the three Article 30 processing activities authored
under issue **#7100**: **PA-31** (the Anthropic-egressing Inngest function fleet), **PA-32**
(community observation and republication) and **PA-33** (the Jikigai-keyed Anthropic API
surface inside GitHub Actions). It exists because counsel review
`knowledge-base/legal/audits/2026-07-counsel-review-7086.md` § Finding 1 found that the
register carried **no entry covering the dominant Jikigai-keyed Anthropic egress surface at
all** — "a live Art. 30(1) incompleteness, not a stylistic one" — and § Finding 3 found that
"there is consequently **no Art. 6 basis of record** for transmitting a Discord member's
message or a Hacker News poster's content to a US processor."

**Two framing points carried over from that review, both load-bearing here.**

1. **The Anthropic DPA cannot supply a lawful basis.** It is an Art. 28 vendor record. A
   lawful basis is a controller-side Art. 6 determination and it lives in the Art. 30 entry
   and in this LIA. Art. 28 coverage is unaffected either way — the DPA auto-incorporates via
   Anthropic's Commercial Terms § C at the account level, not per workload
   (`knowledge-base/legal/data-processing-agreements/anthropic.md`).
2. **Output minimization is not input minimization.** `cron-community-monitor`'s prompt
   directives constrain what the published *digest* may contain. They do not constrain what
   reaches the *model*. The raw Discord messages, GitHub comment bodies and Hacker News
   comment text are in the model input regardless of what the digest says. This distinction
   is stated again, with the verbatim directives and the verbatim counter-directives, in
   §Arm B1 and §Arm B2 below.

**Four arms, because they reach different conclusions.** Arm A (repository / engineering
population), Arm B1 (community collection and egress to Anthropic), Arm B2 (republication of
that community data to a permanent public surface) and Arm C (CI contributors). B1 and B2 are
split because they are **different processing operations under Art. 4(2)** — "disclosure by
transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available" is a distinct operation from
collection and consultation — and because they reach **opposite conclusions**.

## Purpose (common to all four arms)

Jikigai operates Soleur as an agent-native company in which recurring engineering, product,
marketing, legal and community work is executed by scheduled Inngest functions and by CI
jobs that call a large language model. The legitimate interests pursued are:

1. **Operating and maintaining Jikigai's own software** — triage, review, bug-fixing,
   architecture and documentation upkeep, dependency and constraint repair (Arms A and C).
   Recital 47 recognises a controller's interest in the ordinary operation of its business;
   Recital 49 recognises network and information security as a legitimate interest, which is
   the specific interest served by `fix-constraints-stage-a` and the CI gates.
2. **Situational awareness of the community around a public open-source project** — knowing
   what is being asked, reported and discussed so the operator can respond (Arm B1).
3. *(Asserted by the implementation, and assessed and rejected below)* **Producing a
   published digest of that community activity naming individual participants** (Arm B2).

**Operator population.** The Soleur operator (`ops@jikigai.com`) is a data subject of every
arm — their own commits, issue comments, prompts and run telemetry are processed. Their basis
is **Art. 6(1)(b)** (performance of the contract under which the platform is operated for
them), mirroring PA-22's treatment of the operator alongside the third-party limb, with
Art. 6(1)(f) applying to the derived cost- and run-telemetry writes. The operator population
is **not** the difficult question in this LIA and is not further balanced; every balancing
below concerns third parties.

## Arm A — repository and engineering population

**Data subjects.** Commit authors, issue authors, pull-request authors and reviewers whose
content is already published in the public `jikig-ai/soleur` repository.

**Data.** Commit messages and authorship metadata, issue and PR titles and bodies, review
comments, and the source tree itself — read by the agent after spawn, under its own
`Read`/`Glob`/`Grep` grants, and transmitted to Anthropic under the Jikigai key.

**Necessity.** The contributor's content **is the artefact under assessment**. A triage
agent cannot triage an issue whose body it has not read; a review agent cannot review a diff
it has not seen. Anonymisation or aggregation would defeat the purpose rather than reduce
intrusion — this is the same necessity reasoning already accepted for PA-22, where "the
contributor's commit content is the artefact under review — anonymisation would defeat the
purpose". Necessity is satisfied.

**Balancing.** The data subject has already published this content on a public repository
under its licence and under the GitHub Terms of Service. Their Recital 47 reasonable
expectation is that the repository maintainer reads, quotes, triages and responds to it. The
processing adds one recipient (Anthropic PBC, as the maintainer's Art. 28 processor) to an
operation the subject already expects the maintainer to perform. The closest internal
precedent is PA-22's reasonable-expectation analysis for third-party contributor PII in PR
diffs, and PA-7's analysis for CLA signatures ("data is publicly disclosed by the signer
through the act of contributing").

**Two things this arm does NOT get to assume.**

- **There is no PII scrub on this path.** PA-22 records `sanitizePromptString` plus email
  scrubbing at prompt assembly as an Art. 32 measure. **That measure does not exist here**,
  and on the CLI path it structurally could not: the agent reads files itself, after spawn,
  so there is no prompt-assembly chokepoint to scrub at. `redactGithubSourcedText` is applied
  only to the outbound Sentry tail, not to Anthropic-bound content. Importing PA-22's TOM
  into this arm would be a materially false Art. 32 statement.
- **Sentry is a recipient of content here.** `formatTailForSentry`
  (`apps/web-platform/server/inngest/functions/_cron-shared.ts`) ships a bounded
  stdout/stderr tail through `redactGithubSourcedText`, which strips emails, phones, UUIDs,
  IP addresses, JWTs, authorization headers and credential shapes — but **not** usernames,
  handles, display names or free text (`apps/web-platform/lib/safety/redaction-allowlist.ts`,
  which describes its own set as "conservative"). The fleet's stdout is collector JSON
  containing exactly those. PA-27's sentence "Sentry/Better Stack are NOT recipients of
  content" is true for PA-27 and **false here**.

**Conclusion — Arm A: legitimate interest prevails**, on the condition that the record states
the two limitations above rather than inheriting a stronger TOM set from PA-22. Art. 6(1)(f)
is available for the transmission of already-public repository content to Anthropic PBC as
an Art. 28 processor.

## Arm B1 — community collection and egress to Anthropic

**Data subjects.** Members of the Jikigai-operated Discord guild; Hacker News posters; GitHub
issue and PR commenters; GitHub stargazers. All are **involuntary data subjects** in the
PA-27 sense: none of them entered a relationship with Jikigai, and none supplied their data
to Jikigai on a form.

**Data.** Raw Discord guild messages (author, content, timestamps — emitted unprojected by
the collector), Hacker News comment text (untruncated), GitHub comment bodies with a
120-character snippet plus author handle, contributor lists, and stargazer usernames with
starred dates. All of it enters the model input under the Jikigai key.

**Output ≠ input, stated plainly.** The prompt in `cron-community-monitor.ts` carries these
minimization directives verbatim:

> "…only — never list individual followers, commenters, or likers."

> "Summarize and aggregate -- do not store raw message transcripts. Brief contextual quotes
> (under 100 chars) with attribution are acceptable."

**These bind the digest that is written. They do not bind what reaches Anthropic.** The raw
Discord messages, the untruncated Hacker News comment text and the GitHub comment bodies are
in the model's context regardless of what the digest is instructed to contain. That is the
distinction counsel review #7086 § Finding 3 found "legally load-bearing" and found missing
from both amended records. It is stated here, and again in PA-31 §(c) and PA-32 §(c).

Note also that the first directive is scoped, in source, **only to the LinkedIn section** of
the digest — it is not a digest-wide rule — and that the same prompt carries
**counter-directives that mandate** the publication of identifiers. See Arm B2.

**Necessity (for collection and egress only).** The operator's interest is situational
awareness of the community around their public project. Reading what was posted is necessary
to know what was posted. The material question is whether a **less intrusive** collection
would serve the same purpose, and the honest answer is **partly**: projecting at the
collector boundary — emitting `{author_id_hash, channel, timestamp, length}` plus a link
rather than the raw message object — would materially reduce what reaches Anthropic while
preserving the operator's awareness of activity level and location. That measure does not
exist today. It is a genuine Art. 25(1) data-protection-by-design gap and is tracked as
deferred item **DEF-3** (#7124; input-side minimization for the community collectors; body drafted
at `knowledge-base/project/specs/feat-one-shot-7100-art30-eval-fleet-register/deferred-issues.md`,
).

Necessity is nevertheless **satisfied for the collection-and-egress limb as scoped**, because
the operator's purpose does require reading the substance of what was said (a hashed activity
count would not tell the operator that a member reported a bug), and because the egress
recipient is a contracted Art. 28 processor rather than a further disclosure to the world.

**Balancing.**

- *Nature of the data.* Business- and project-context communications, but including
  **semi-private** content: the Discord guild is readable only with `DISCORD_BOT_TOKEN`. It
  is not a publicly accessible source. Hacker News and GitHub comments are public.
- *Reasonable expectations (Recital 47).* A person who comments on a public GitHub issue
  reasonably expects the maintainer to read it and to use tooling — including AI tooling — to
  triage it. A person posting in a bot-token-gated Discord guild that Jikigai operates
  reasonably expects Jikigai to read the message. Neither expectation is strained by the
  message being read **by the maintainer's contracted processor** rather than by the
  maintainer's own eyes, provided the processor is bound by Art. 28 terms and the retention
  is disclosed. It **is** strained by publication, which is Arm B2 and is assessed
  separately.
- *Impact.* Bounded by the Art. 28 relationship, but real: the Zero-Retention amendment is
  **unsigned** (`knowledge-base/legal/data-processing-agreements/anthropic.md` frontmatter
  `zero_retention_amendment: unsigned`), so Anthropic's default **30-day** API
  request/response retention applies to this content at a US processor. The self-hosted
  Inngest event/run store retains step payloads and run logs with **no automatic deletion**,
  as already recorded for PA-27.
- *Safeguards actually in place.* The Art. 28 DPA with DPF + SCCs Modules 2+3 + UK IDTA +
  Swiss Addendum; the Tier-2 nftables egress firewall enforced outside the agent's process
  tree; the per-cron explicit environment allowlist; the ephemeral per-run workspace with a
  GC backstop; the deny-by-default `PreToolUse` hook (on 13 of 15 CLI members — see
  Provenance); and the spawn-time `runHookSelfTest` that aborts the run if the hook is
  unreachable.
- *Safeguards NOT in place, named rather than implied.* No PII scrub on Anthropic-bound
  content. No input-side projection at the collectors. No Art. 14 notice to this population
  (see §Art. 14 below). No erasure route for content already sent (the 30-day window simply
  expires). No mechanical guard tying the code fleet to the register.

**Conclusion — Arm B1: legitimate interest prevails, narrowly and conditionally.** Art.
6(1)(f) is available for collecting community content and transmitting it to Anthropic PBC as
an Art. 28 processor for the operator's situational awareness. The conclusion is
**conditional on the Art. 14 obligation being discharged** (it is currently overdue — see
below) and would be re-opened by: the Zero-Retention amendment remaining unsigned 90 days
from this record's date; any collector being pointed at a guild, repository or account other
than Jikigai's own; or any expansion beyond single-operator scale.

## Arm B2 — republication of community data to a permanent public surface

**This arm reaches the opposite conclusion from Arm B1, and it is the most consequential
finding in this record.**

**The operation.** `cron-community-monitor` writes a digest file into
`knowledge-base/support/community/` in the **public** `jikig-ai/soleur` repository, and
`cron-daily-triage` writes `gh issue comment` bodies onto **public** GitHub issues. Both are
distinct operations under Art. 4(2) — "disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise
making available" — performed on data collected under Arm B1. Per the EDPB's legitimate-
interests guidance, a new context requires a fresh balancing; this is that balancing.

**This is not a prospective risk. It has materialised, at scale, and part of it is live.**
Per the Provenance section: 80 committed digests from 2026-02-19 to 2026-06-08; 45 carrying
the `| User | Issue/PR | Comment |` table of handles and comment snippets; 65 naming
stargazers by username and date; named Discord members with handles and inferred
affiliation, including a verbatim quoted private-guild message attributed to a handle; zero
deletions ever; a public repository with two forks. The digests were committed by
`claude[bot]` and `soleur-ai[bot]` under auto-merge, with **no human gate**. The
public-issue-comment limb is live now; the file-write limb appears not to have produced a
digest since 2026-06-08. The limb is nonetheless **live and scheduled** — `cron-community-monitor` is registered on a daily `0 8 * * *` schedule with its write path unchanged — so the absence of recent output is a persistence observation, not dormancy, and the cause is not established *(stated flatly 2026-07-31 at review; this previously read "its live state should be re-verified at merge", which deferred a determinable fact and embedded an unperformed operator step)* — that reduces
nothing legally, because the 80 published digests remain published.

**The prompt does not merely permit this — it mandates it.** Alongside the minimization
directives quoted in Arm B1, the same prompt carries these **counter-directives**:

> "…new stargazers in the period (username and starred date) from the repo-stats data."

> "…sub-section with a markdown table: `| User | Issue/PR | Comment |`. Each row shows…"

An instruction to list stargazer usernames, and an instruction to tabulate commenter handles
with comment snippets, are the direct opposite of "never list individual followers,
commenters, or likers" — which, as measured, is scoped in source only to the LinkedIn section
of the digest. A record that quoted the minimization directives without quoting these would
misdescribe the system.

**Necessity fails, and it is dispositive.** The purpose of the digest is **operator awareness
of community activity**. Naming the stargazer, naming and quoting the Discord member, and
tabulating commenter handles with snippets is **not necessary** to that purpose: an aggregate
count ("7 new stargazers; 4 issue comments across 3 issues") plus a link to the source issue
serves the operator's awareness identically. Art. 6(1)(f)'s necessity limb is strict — the
CJEU held in **C-13/16 *Rīgas satiksme*** (judgment of 4 May 2017, on the materially
identical Art. 7(f) of Directive 95/46/EC) that "necessity" is an autonomous concept requiring
that the interest cannot reasonably be achieved by less intrusive means, and EDPB Guidelines
1/2024 on Art. 6(1)(f) treat necessity as a cumulative requirement that is assessed **before**
and **independently of** balancing. Where a less intrusive means achieves the purpose,
legitimate interest is unavailable **regardless of how the balancing would come out**.

**Balancing would fail anyway on the Discord limb.** The guild is bot-token-gated and is not
a publicly accessible source. A member's Recital 47 reasonable expectation is that their
message stays in the guild — not that it is quoted verbatim, attributed to their handle, and
committed to a world-readable git repository that has been forked twice.

**Art. 17 is not merely unimplemented — it is not implementable.** Git history is append-only.
Deleting a digest in a new commit erases nothing: the data persists in every prior commit
object, in GitHub's commit UI, in every clone, and in **both forks**. `git filter-repo` plus a
force-push breaks downstream clones, **does not reach forks**, and does not reach public issue
bodies (GitHub retains issue edit history). There is therefore no route by which a Discord
member or a GitHub commenter can have their handle or quoted comment removed from what has
been published.

**The decisive internal precedent: Jikigai has already decided this question against itself.**
PA-30's lawful-basis analysis justifies the beta-CRM's structured database store precisely on
the ground that the alternative is *"git-committed PII, an Art. 17 impossibility"*, and the
accompanying LIA is explicit: *"Third-party PII is stored in per-tenant Supabase Postgres
tables under owner-only RLS — **never committed to git**. Git-committed third-party PII would
be an Art. 17 erasure impossibility (git history is permanent)… The DB boundary is
load-bearing, not incidental."*
(`knowledge-base/legal/legitimate-interest-assessments/2026-07-07-beta-crm-lia.md`, §Necessity
¶1.) `cron-community-monitor` does exactly the thing Jikigai rejected in writing for a
different activity — and does it on a *public* repository rather than into a private table. A
supervisory authority reading PA-30 and PA-32 side by side finds the inconsistency
immediately, and it is not defensible to resolve it by asserting a basis here that was denied
there.

**Conclusion — Arm B2: legitimate interest does NOT prevail. Art. 6(1)(f) is not available
for the republication limb as implemented.** The assessment fails at the **necessity** limb
and does not reach balancing; had it reached balancing, the Discord limb would fail there
too. No other Art. 6 basis is available: there is no contract with these data subjects
(6(1)(b)), no consent has been sought (6(1)(a)), and no legal obligation, vital interest or
public-task ground applies. **The honest record is "no available lawful basis, with tracked
remediation" — and that is what PA-32 records.**

**Conditions under which this conclusion would change.** The remediation set is small, and

## Arm C — CI contributors

**Data subjects.** Authors of same-repo pull requests to `jikig-ai/soleur` whose branch head
is checked out and read by an agent in GitHub Actions.

**Data.** Contributor-authored source at the PR head SHA, PR metadata (title, branch, SHA),
and — for the two `ci.yml` gate jobs — sandbox and plugin-propagation probe inputs. Fork PRs
receive no secrets, so `.github/actions/anthropic-preflight/action.yml` fails and the agent
step is skipped; the exposure is bounded to same-repo branches.

**Necessity.** `fix-constraints-stage-a` exists to repair a red dependency-cruiser
import-boundary gate on the PR that broke it; it cannot do so without reading that PR's tree.
The two `ci.yml` jobs make one paid model turn each to verify a sandbox capture and a plugin-
root propagation invariant. In each case the contributor's code **is** the subject matter.
Necessity is satisfied.

**Balancing.** A contributor who opens a pull request against a public repository with
published contribution terms does so knowingly and expects automated processing of the
branch: CI is the visible, universal norm, and this repository additionally requires a CLA
before merge. Their Recital 47 expectation comfortably covers a CI job reading the branch.
The processing is bounded by `permissions: contents: read` on the agent job, a PR-head-SHA
checkout with `persist-credentials: false`, and the fork exclusion.

**Honest limitations to record.** `fix-constraints-stage-a` is **path-filtered** at the
trigger (`apps/web-platform/app/**`, `components/**`, `server/**`, and the two
dependency-cruiser config files), but once triggered the agent's `Read`/`Grep` reach the
**whole** checked-out tree, not only the changed paths. And `ci.yml` fires on
`push:[main]`, `pull_request` **and** `merge_group` — so the two paid Anthropic calls are not
PR-only, and the record must not describe PA-33 as a PR-only surface.

**CLA coverage — an open question, not a resolved one.** PA-7 records the Individual and
Corporate CLA as the mechanism by which contributors grant Jikigai an IP licence. Whether
that grant extends to **transmitting contributor-authored source to a third-party AI
processor** is a contract-scope question that the CLA text was not written to answer, and it
is a distinct question from the GDPR basis assessed here (an IP licence is not a lawful basis
and a lawful basis is not an IP licence). This LIA does **not** resolve it; it is recorded as
outstanding counsel-review item 3 below and cross-referenced from PA-33.

**Conclusion — Arm C: legitimate interest prevails.** Art. 6(1)(f) is available for
processing same-repo contributor source and PR metadata through the Jikigai-keyed CI surface,
subject to the CLA-scope question remaining open and to the Art. 14 posture below (which for
this arm is materially better than for Arm B).

## Art. 14 — assessed per arm

Art. 14 applies wherever personal data has **not** been obtained from the data subject. It
applies here to Arms A, B1, B2 and C alike, but the conclusion differs per arm, and a single
monolithic statement would be wrong in both directions.

**Timing, and why the posture is "overdue" rather than "open".** Art. 14(3)(a) requires the
information to be provided within a reasonable period and at the latest **within one month**
of obtaining the data. The earliest committed digest is
`knowledge-base/support/community/2026-02-19-digest.md`. The obligation in respect of that
cohort has therefore been unmet since approximately **2026-03-19**, and it continues to
accrue on each run of the live public-issue limb. Recording this as merely "open" would
understate it exactly as counsel review #7086 found "framing not yet assessed" understated
the Art. 30 gap.

| Arm | Population | Art. 14 status |

| **A** | Repository contributors, issue/PR authors | **Engaged; substantially discharged by context, not by a notice.** The repository's own public surfaces (README, CONTRIBUTING, issue templates) are the natural channel and a line there closes it. Lowest-cost remediation of the four. |
| **B1 / B2** | Discord guild members, GitHub commenters, Hacker News posters, stargazers | **Engaged, UNDISCHARGED and OVERDUE since ~2026-03-19.** No Art. 14(5) exemption is claimed — see the exemption table below. |
| **C** | Same-repo CI contributors | **Engaged; materially satisfied by context.** Contributors open PRs knowingly against a repository with published contribution terms and visible CI; the Art. 14(1) information is nevertheless best stated explicitly in CONTRIBUTING. |

**No Art. 14(5) exemption is claimed for the community population.** Art. 14(5)(a) (the
subject already has the information) is unavailable — the digests and the model egress are
not disclosed anywhere the subject would see. Art. 14(5)(b) (disproportionate effort) is
**not established** for two of the three sub-populations:

| Sub-population | Art. 14(5)(b) disproportionate effort? |
|---|---|
| Discord guild members | **Fails.** Jikigai *operates the guild*. A pinned notice or a server-description line costs essentially nothing, so the effort is not disproportionate. |

**Remediation is multi-channel, not one Privacy Policy section**, precisely because
