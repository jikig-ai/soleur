## The two rulings put to this review

**Ruling 1 — 4 indexed / 3 waived, not 7/0. CONFIRMED as to method; the *set* is wrong.**

The method is right and the plan's was not. A regex over `4\(12\)|33\(5\)|Art\. 33` produces
files, not determinations, and applying a conjunctive semantic predicate to that output is the
correct move. Each of the three original waivers holds against its source, verified line by line:

- `audits/2026-08-counsel-review-7440.md` — an express Art. 4(12) analysis, but of a *prospective*
  PA-8 amendment ("Nothing here engages Art. 33 or Art. 34"; "this was the addition of a recipient
  role, not an incident"). Limb 1 fails on its face. Waiver **UPHELD**, and the ground given —
  that indexing prospective clearances would make the register indistinguishable from a change log

- `audits/2026-05-12-gdpr-gate-plan-phase-2-7-outcome.md` — the non-notifiable statement is
  expressly qualified "under the operator's framing", and the matter is a disclosure-timing lag on
  a flag flip, not a security event. Waiver **UPHELD** on limb 1; the "never cites Art. 4(12)"
  observation is true but is **not** the operative ground, and should not be read as one (see B1).

The three INDEX calls are also confirmed, including the close one. Indexing
`audits/2026-08-06-alpha-tester-controller-processor-determination.md` is right for the reason
given: Art. 33(5) exists so an authority can verify determinations concluding *no* notification,
and omitting the corpus's closest-run call is the omission that would cost credibility. Recording
the preserved counter-argument in the Art. 33 / Art. 34 cells rather than flattening it to "No" is
correct and must not be tidied.

Where the ruling is wrong is in its scope: the set was drawn before the producer pattern was
widened at review, and the two files that widening surfaced were parked as "pending" rather than
ruled. They are ruled in B1 below.

**Ruling 2 — `controller:`, not `processor:`. CONFIRMED without qualification.**

Art. 33(1) and 33(5) bind "the controller" on their face. A processor's obligation is the
Art. 33(2) duty to notify its controller without undue delay, and that duty carries no register
of its own. The Art. 30(2) register carries `processor:` because that field names the **capacity
in which the register is kept** — not because register frontmatter takes one field or the other.
Deriving the field by diffing two existing files' field lists was the error; deriving it from the
capacity the obligation attaches to is the fix. AC1's other prohibitions (`version`,
`last_reviewed`, `dpo`, `contact`) correctly stand.

One point worth stating for the next reader, because it is the obvious objection: the 2026-08-06
determination is a **dual finding**, processor for one limb and controller for another, and it is
indexed in a controller-capacity register. That is right, and the register already says why — the
Art. 4(12) finding addresses the controller limb (PA-34, PA-35), while the processor limb's record
is P-1 in the Art. 30(2) register. Nothing further is needed.

## B1 — BLOCKING. The inclusion predicate is applied inconsistently, and a determination is outside the index

**The ruling: limb 2 is substantive, not citational.** A determination is an assessment of whether

## What was verified and found sound

Stated so a later reader knows what this review actually checked rather than assumed.

- **The instrument choice (ADR-200 part 1).** Art. 30(1)'s limbs are a closed list and breach

  item 12 and is properly left open there.
- **Index-not-transcription (ADR-200 part 2).** Correct, and the third ground is the strongest and
  the least obvious: a register entry is a live representation to a supervisory authority, so
  reproducing a determination's evidentiary sentence represents that the controller *currently*
  believes it — which a later narrowing annotation may have made false. Transcription would have
  manufactured misrepresentations the originals do not carry.
- **Separate Art. 33 and Art. 34 columns, and recording silence as silence.** The 2026-08-17 row's
  "Not stated in source" is right and must not be tidied to "No"; writing "No" would mint a
  finding nobody made.
- **The 2026-06-29 row** is accurate against its source and both addenda, including the two hard
  parts: that `edge_logs` is uninstrumented so its zero was never evidence, and that
  `postgrest_logs` — the source that actually records REST traffic — was never queried and the
  window is recorded as *not established to be recoverable* rather than as unrecoverable. Claiming
  the stronger negative would have been an inference drawn from the instrument the same addendum

- **PA-8 §(g)'s Art. 33 evidentiary-chain limitation, and the §Non-scope refusal to record a
  retention measure for the Supabase log surface.** Both correct. What was measured there is

- **PA-8 §(d)'s re-grounding of the no-notification conclusion.** The withdrawn ground ("under
  processor-DPA terms") is falsified by this same PR, and the conclusion is correctly re-grounded

  an Art. 28(3) instrument was executed; the instrument gap is a lawfulness-and-documentation
  defect of a different class, and #7529 is the right home for it.
- **Deferring the published `docs/legal/` copies to #7786.** Sound. The three-way `docs/legal/**`
  lockstep and a possible `TC_VERSION` bump must not ride inside a statutory-register change, and
