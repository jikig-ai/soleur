## 2. Conclusions

### 2.1 PA-31 (internal egress) and PA-33 (CI surface) — full DPIA not required

For PA-31, two criteria are engaged (innovative technology; vulnerable/involuntary subjects,
partially) — the same shape PA-27 assessed, and WP248's own guidance is that a DPIA is
normally indicated "in most cases" where two criteria are met. That is a presumption, not a
rule, and it is displaced here for three reasons specific to these two activities: there is
no public-output limb (that limb is PA-32); the recipient is a contracted Art. 28 processor
under DPF + SCCs Modules 2+3 + UK IDTA + Swiss Addendum; and the scale is one operator's own
repository at solo-company volume. For PA-33 a single criterion is engaged and the subjects
initiated the processing themselves by opening a pull request.

**This screening, the LIA and the register entries are the proportionate Art. 24/25 record for
PA-31 and PA-33**, with the residuals in §3 accepted **by name** rather than mitigated away.

### 2.2 PA-32 (community observation and republication) — a full DPIA IS required, and it is overdue

Six WP248 criteria are engaged, three times the threshold at which WP248 indicates a DPIA. The
combination is the point: **systematically monitored** private and public communications,
**combined across platforms** beyond the subject's expectation, concerning people who have
**no relationship with the controller and no notice**, processed by **autonomous tooling with a
publication path and no human gate**, in a manner that **forecloses Art. 17, Art. 15/20 and
Art. 21**. That is a high risk to rights and freedoms in the Art. 35(1) sense, and the "large
scale" miss does not rescue it: Art. 35(1) does not require large scale, only high risk.

**Art. 35(1) requires the assessment to be carried out "prior to the processing".** The
earliest committed digest is `knowledge-base/support/community/2026-02-19-digest.md`, so the
assessment is **overdue by approximately five months**, and the public-issue-comment limb
continues to run. This is recorded as a compliance finding, not as a plan.

The full DPIA is tracked as deferred item **DEF-5** (#7121; body drafted at
`knowledge-base/project/specs/feat-one-shot-7100-art30-eval-fleet-register/deferred-issues.md`;
GitHub issue number wired at filing). PA-32 §(g) records `DPIA status: full DPIA required —
overdue since ~2026-02-19; scoped in DEF-5`.

### 2.3 Remediation is very likely the better path, and the memo says so

The LIA reaches an independent conclusion that Art. 6(1)(f) is **not available** for the
republication limb as implemented, because it fails at the necessity limb — an aggregate
count plus a source link serves the digest's purpose identically without naming anyone
(`2026-07-31-claude-eval-fleet-and-ci-lia.md` §Arm B2). If remediation **R1–R4** lands
(drop the mandated stargazer username list; replace the `| User | Issue/PR | Comment |`
table with a link and a count; delete the attributed-quotation licence; add a code-level
redaction pass before persistence), then of the six engaged criteria:

| Criterion | Effect of R1–R4 |
|---|---|
| 4 — data of a highly personal nature | **Collapses.** No private-guild message content is published or quoted. |
| 6 — matching/combining | **Collapses.** An aggregate activity count identifies no one, so no cross-platform record of an individual is created. |
| 9 — forecloses a right | **Collapses prospectively.** Nothing identifying is newly committed to git history. (It does **not** collapse retrospectively — see residual (c).) |
| 7 — vulnerable subjects | **Substantially reduced**, and fully addressed only once Art. 14 notice (DEF-1) and an Art. 21(1) route (DEF-9) exist. |
| 3 — systematic monitoring | Unchanged — collection continues. Reduced further by DEF-3 (collector-side projection). |
| 8 — innovative technology | Unchanged. |

That leaves two criteria — the PA-31 shape — for which this screening is the proportionate
record. **Conducting a full DPIA on a limb the controller's own LIA concludes has no lawful
basis is the less useful of the two paths**; the assessment would be assessing something that
should not be happening. Counsel should decide between (i) commissioning the full DPIA now
and (ii) landing R1–R4 first and re-running this screening, with the strong caveat that
option (ii) does **not** cure the five-month overdue period nor the already-published corpus,
and that **the processing should not continue unremediated while either path is pending**.

## 3. Named accepted residual risks

Accepted **by name**, not mitigated away on paper. Each has an owner-visible failure mode and
a stated recovery path — or an honest statement that none exists.

**(a) Art. 9 content arriving unsolicited in free text and reaching Anthropic, and possibly
the published digest.** A Discord message, a Hacker News comment or a GitHub issue body may
contain health, belief, political or other Art. 9 content; the collectors apply no content
filter, and — unlike PA-27 — there is **no closed output allowlist** and **no
system-prompt omission instruction** on the digest path. PA-27's own residual (a) records
that "a prompt instruction is a claim, not a mechanism"; here there is not even the claim.
*Accepted because:* the volume is small, the sources are technical-project channels where
such content is not expected, and the Anthropic-side copy expires with the 30-day window.
*Not accepted for the published limb* — a leaked Art. 9 disclosure in a committed digest is
subject to the same Art. 17 impossibility as everything else in residual (c). *Re-screening
trigger:* first observed Art. 9 content in a published digest or issue comment.

**(b) Prompt injection from attacker-controllable input to public publication.** Anyone may
post in the guild, comment on an issue or post to Hacker News. That text becomes model input,
and the model's output **auto-merges to a public repository and auto-publishes as a public
GitHub issue comment, with no human gate and no output allowlist**. PA-27 bounded its
equivalent residual with "no tools + a closed `MAIL_CLASS_ALLOWLIST`"; **here neither bound
exists and a publication tool does.** This is a genuine Art. 32(1)(b) integrity finding, not
merely a quality risk: the blast radius is not a misleading summary but attacker-chosen text
on a public surface under Jikigai's bot identity. *Accepted only as an interim posture*,
because it overlaps R4 and is separately tracked as deferred item **DEF-7** (#7122). *Re-screening
trigger:* first observed injected content reaching a published artefact — or immediately, if
counsel assesses the absence of a human gate as unacceptable at any volume.

**(c) The already-published corpus cannot be erased.** 80 digests, 45 with the commenter
table, 65 naming stargazers, on a public repository with two forks, with zero deletions ever.
No remediation reaches this: R1–R4 govern future runs only. *There is no recovery path.* The
honest options are a documented finding that erasure is impossible and a decision on whether
the corpus is left in place, or a repository-history rewrite that breaks downstream clones and
still does not reach the forks or GitHub's retained issue-edit history. Posed, not answered,
as R5 / deferred item **DEF-4** (#7119), and as outstanding counsel-review item 2 in the LIA.

**(d) Two of the fifteen CLI members run with no containment hook.** `cron-daily-triage` and
`cron-follow-through-monitor` never call `setupEphemeralWorkspace`, so they get no
`PreToolUse` hook, no `CRON_BASH_ALLOWLISTS` entry, no `runHookSelfTest` and no ephemeral
workspace; the substrate's own committed probe evidence records that `--allowedTools` alone
does **not** fail-close. Their sole compensating control is the Tier-2 nftables egress
firewall, which is enforced outside the agent's process tree but is destination-only and
content-blind. Whether those two run under the CLI's own sandbox is **not determinable from
source**, and this memo asserts it in neither direction. *Accepted as an interim posture*;
tracked as deferred item **DEF-6** (#7123). *Re-screening trigger:* a third member losing its hook, or
either of these two gaining a new write path.

**(e) The register snapshot decays with no mechanical guard.** PA-31's 21-member snapshot and
PA-33's 5-member snapshot are dated statements of fact with nothing asserting them in CI. The
repository already contains the counter-example: `scripts/check-pa-22.sh` was written to guard
the PA-22 entry and is wired into no workflow (`git grep check-pa-22 .github/` returns zero
hits). *Accepted only because the fix is out of this record's scope*; tracked as deferred item
**DEF-2** (#7125), which folds `check-pa-22.sh`'s single assertion into the same sweep. *Re-screening
trigger:* before the next new Anthropic-egressing function lands.

## 4. Why the remaining surface does not change the conclusions

- **Anthropic's 30-day default retention.** The Zero-Retention amendment is **unsigned**
  (`knowledge-base/legal/data-processing-agreements/anthropic.md`), so every Jikigai-keyed
  request and response — including raw Discord message content and Hacker News comment text —
  rests at a US processor for up to 30 days. Bounded, short, disclosed, and covered by DPF +
  SCCs Modules 2+3 + UK IDTA + Swiss Addendum. It raises the impact of PA-31 without changing
  its criteria tally; it is a re-evaluation trigger at 90 days unsigned.
- **The self-hosted Inngest event/run store.** Step payloads and run logs land in the same
  operator-managed, localhost-bound store already recorded for PA-27, which performs **no
  automatic deletion**. A PII surface on EU infrastructure inside the same trust boundary —
  not a third-party recipient, and not a new criterion.
- **Sentry as a recipient of a bounded tail.** `formatTailForSentry` ships a stdout/stderr
  tail through `redactGithubSourcedText`, which strips emails, phones, UUIDs, IPs, JWTs and
  credential shapes but **not** usernames, handles, display names or free text. PA-27's
  "Sentry/Better Stack are NOT recipients of content" is true for PA-27 and false here, which
  is why PA-31 §(d) names Sentry. It adds an intra-EU recipient of a bounded tail, not a new
  risk class.
- **Publication is not a Chapter V transfer.** Making the digest world-readable is **not** a
  third-country transfer (*Bodil Lindqvist*, C-101/01, 6 November 2003 — the concept of
  transfer does not cover loading data onto an internet page even where it becomes accessible
  from third countries). The Chapter V limb is the Anthropic egress, and the two must not be
  conflated. This narrows Chapter V exposure; it does not narrow the Art. 5(1)(e) / Art. 17
  finding, which is the operative one for PA-32.
- **Fork exclusion on the CI surface.** Fork pull requests receive no secrets, so the
  preflight fails and the agent step is skipped. PA-33's exposure is bounded to same-repo
  branches — which is why one criterion, not more, is engaged there.
