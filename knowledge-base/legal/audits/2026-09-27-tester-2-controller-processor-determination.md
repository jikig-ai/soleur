  This is the limb the runbook's "designed to stay Posture A" parenthetical does not reach. See §3.

  per the LIA recommendation (no collaborator access — Posture C is designed out, §5), and PA-30 /

## 2. The fact pattern — what the designed hosted path actually does

Per `knowledge-base/engineering/operations/runbooks/alpha-tester-onboarding.md` Step 5, the guided
hosted session does, in order:

1. **Signup** — invite link → accept terms → name workspace. *(Jikigai-controller limb.)*
2. **Anthropic key** — the tester creates their own key at console.anthropic.com and pastes it into
   `/setup-key`; the platform encrypts it at rest. Their credential, their Anthropic Commercial
   Terms relationship. *(The inference chain stays the tester's — the Posture A design point.)*
3. **GitHub connect** — the tester connects their repository. **Repository content — including the
   third-party personal data any business repository carries (commit-author names and email
   addresses at minimum, fixture and record data potentially) — is thereafter processed on
   Jikigai-operated infrastructure.**
4. **First real question → one artifact** — agent runs against the connected workspace under the
   tester's key.

Step 3 is the load-bearing step for this determination. It is not an edge case or a contingency —
it is the designed act of onboarding.

## 3. The trigger-1 question, answered

Posture B trigger 1 of the prior determination reads:

> The tester connects his repository to the **hosted platform** (app.soleur.ai), such that
> repository content is processed on Jikigai-operated infrastructure.

For tester #1 (self-hosted CLI) this trigger never fired — no content reached the platform. For
tester #2 it fires **by design, not by accident**: the runbook's GitHub-connect step is a verbatim
instance of the trigger. The runbook's repair-checklist claim that the hosted path is "designed to
stay Posture A" is accurate **only for limb (i)** — the operator-assisted session, where the
machine/key/purpose triple is the tester's. It does not describe the platform-hosting limb, which
was never part of the machine/key/purpose table because the 2026-08-06 determination was scoped to
operator-assisted runs. The correction is recorded so the claim cannot be re-read as covering
hosting (and the checklist line is corrected alongside this document).

**The instrument position on the platform's own terms.** `docs/legal/terms-and-conditions.md`
§3b.1 allocates controllership of workspace content to the Workspace Owner and states:

> Where and to the extent Jikigai instead processes workspace content on the Workspace Owner's
> documented instructions, it does so as a processor within the meaning of Article 4(8) GDPR. …
> no such instrument is in force between Jikigai and any Workspace Owner today, and Jikigai will
> agree one with the Owner before carrying out processing in that capacity.

Serving a connected workspace *is* processing workspace content on the owner's documented
instructions — the Terms themselves are the documentation. The published position therefore commits
Jikigai to an Art. 28(3) instrument **before** this processing begins, and records that none
exists. `knowledge-base/legal/tenant-dpa-register.md` corroborates: `status:
draft-empty-template`, signed-row count 0. `docs/legal/data-protection-disclosure.md` §2.1c states
the same instrument-first posture for operator-assisted processing.

**Why the tester is a controller here, not merely a data subject.** A founder connecting a business
repository acts in a business capacity: the repository carries third-party personal data (commit
identities at minimum) that the venture determines the purposes and means of. The household
carve-out does not apply; the venture is the controller and Jikigai is the processor for the
hosting limb. This is the ordinary B2B-SaaS allocation — and it is why the missing instrument is a
**platform-wide gap that tester #2 merely surfaces first**, not a defect in the onboarding design.
Every hosted business user presents the same posture; #7468 already tracks the cure.

**The alternative framing, considered and not adopted.** "Ordinary self-serve use is Jikigai's own
controller processing under privacy-policy §4.7 (Art. 6(1)(b) contract performance), so no
instrument is needed." Not adopted: §3b.1 expressly assigns workspace-content controllership to the
Owner and reserves processor status to Jikigai wherever it processes that content on instructions —
the two readings cannot both hold, and the Terms' own text is the one that binds. Where the corpus
contradicts itself the instrument-first sentence is the safer, and the one already published to
users. Recorded so external counsel can overrule with reasons rather than re-derive.

## 4. Why the 2026-08-06 annex is not the cure

The drafted Art. 28(3) instrument
(`knowledge-base/legal/2026-08-06-alpha-tester-processing-annex.md`) was scoped deliberately, per
condition C2, *"as governing operator-assisted runs specifically, not as a generic platform DPA."*
Tester #2's gap is the generic platform-DPA gap: it is hosting and serving workspace content, not
an operator-assisted Jikigai-keyed run, that needs the instrument. Re-scoping the annex to cover
hosting would contradict C2's drafting instruction and would still need the §12 counsel review
before it is sent to anyone.

The instruments that actually close D2:

- **(a) The customer-facing platform DPA** — the instrument ToS §3b.4 already contemplates
  ("Once Jikigai publishes a customer-facing Data Processing Agreement for organizational tenants,
  that instrument supersedes…") and #7468 tracks as the inline Art. 28(3) cure, flagged as the
  corpus's strongest external-counsel re-review candidate. Correct instrument class; scales to
  every hosted user, not just tester #2.
- **(b) A bilateral Art. 28(3) instrument with tester #2** scoped to hosted workspace processing —
  new drafting (the annex's operator-assisted scope cannot be borrowed), and per §12 of the prior
  determination it must be **counsel-reviewed before it is sent**.

Either route lands on the same counsel-review surface as #7468 and #7348, which consolidates the
spend rather than doubling it — see D7.

instrument is a bounded deliverable, not because the fallback fails.
