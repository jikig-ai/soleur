## 1. Finding at attestation — one blocking defect, found and fixed before signing

**This attestation would have been BLOCKED had the diff been signed as presented.** Reading the diff
surfaced a defect that no gate caught and that neither review panel reported.

**The defect.** `privacy-policy.md` §4.2 stated, of granted repository access, that *"it involves
neither our machine nor our API key."* That is the **Adjudication A3 claim**, which PA-35(g)(4)
falsifies: *"Local clones of the observed repository sit on the operator workstation, which is not
full-disk encrypted … the exposure is standing rather than session-bound."* Jikigai reading a
granted-access repository happens on a Jikigai machine.

**Why it was worse than the original A3 instance.** This same delta added, two documents over, a
gdpr-policy §3.14 safeguards clause reading *"the workstation on which any local working copy sits is
**not** full-disk encrypted."* So the corpus as presented asserted, in one delta, both that
granted-access reading involves no Jikigai machine and that it involves a Jikigai workstation holding
local copies. A direct internal contradiction between two published documents, introduced by the
change that was supposed to remove it.

**Mechanism — worth recording, because it defeated three separate controls.** Adjudication B's edit
deleted the D5-b paragraph by over-matching regex. The paragraph was then restored *verbatim from the
commit where it correctly landed* — a commit that **predates Adjudication A3**. The restore therefore
silently reverted A3 for that paragraph. It survived because: (i) the A3 checklist rows enumerated
only `data-protection-disclosure.md` and `gdpr-policy.md`, since those were the sites I named — my
under-enumeration; (ii) the privacy-policy phrasing is *"neither our machine nor our API key"*, not
*"no Jikigai machine"*, so it was invisible to the A3 greps by wording as well as by file; and (iii)
a restore-to-green looks like a repair, so it attracts no re-review.

**Fix applied at attestation**, on both surfaces, as a direct application of the existing A3 ruling
rather than a new one:

> …it does not require you to ask us for anything, **the reading does not by itself send your content
> to our AI provider under our credentials**, and it continues between sessions for as long as the
> access lasts.

`privacy-policy` SHA repinned (`b1c544f3…` → `dc2df0ce…`). `check-tc-document-sha.sh` rc=0;
`legal-doc-consistency` 13/13. **The corrected diff is what is attested below.**

**Standing lesson for the record:** a restore-from-an-earlier-commit reverts every ruling made between
that commit and HEAD. It must be treated as a new edit requiring re-verification against the current
ruling set, not as a return to a known-good state.
