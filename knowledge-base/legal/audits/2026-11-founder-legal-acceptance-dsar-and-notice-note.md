# DSAR, erasure reply and notice text — founder legal-acceptance record (PA-40)

Scope: tables `legal_acceptance` and `legal_reauth_event` (migration 184). Data subject class at this stage: the Jikigai operator only. Text below marked "proposed" is not approved for sending or display until counsel has decided the open items it depends on (Register Maintenance item 17).

## (a) DSAR treatment

**Self-serve export: both tables excluded.** Both are in `DSAR_TABLE_EXCLUSIONS` (the code change is in PR-B), not in `DSAR_TABLE_ALLOWLIST`. Stated reasons, to be copied into the exclusion entry:

1. The founder id is a **pseudonymous uuid** and the row is **kept after account deletion under Art. 17(3)(e)**, so the export keyed on the account cannot be the complete route: after erasure there is no account to key it on.
2. The export worker reads with `service_role`, which holds no privilege on either table (migration 184 revokes it). Putting a table in the allowlist without a grant or a read function would make every export fail.

**Access remains available on request (Art. 15).** Route: email <legal@jikigai.com>. Handling, proposed:

- *Account still exists.* Request is sent from the email address on the account. Jikigai reads the founder's rows through the database owner role by user id and sends them, including `requested_edit` and the time and choice of each decision. Statutory clock and handling: `knowledge-base/legal/statutory-response-catalog.md#dsar`. The founder can also see their own `legal_acceptance` rows in the product once PR-C is live (the row-level policy allows it); the email route is the fallback and the route for `legal_reauth_event`, which the founder cannot read.
- *Account already deleted.* The row carries only a uuid. Jikigai cannot assume that a requester is the person behind it. The requester is asked to give the user id or other facts that tie the request to the account (for example, the date of the decision and the text concerned); Jikigai matches against the `text_hash` and `created_at` and confirms identity with additional information under Art. 12(6). If matching is not possible, Jikigai says so in reply and relies on Art. 11. Whether this is adequate is a counsel question (item 17(d)).
- *Content of the copy.* The `legal_acceptance` row fields in the Art. 30 register (c) list, without the checksum (it is an internal integrity value; supplied if asked), plus any `legal_reauth_event` rows still held for the user (the same list, less the code, which is never stored).

> **Why.** We need to be able to show later what was decided on which text, in case a legal claim arises. This is a legitimate interest of ours (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) and the law allows keeping data needed for legal claims even after an erasure request (Article 17(3)(e) GDPR). Because the identifier is not anonymous (someone who holds other records of ours could link it to you), we treat it as personal data.

## (c) Notice for the Request changes field (exact text, proposed)

> **Say only what you want changed in the text. Please do not write names, email addresses or private details about yourself or anyone else here: this note is saved with your decision, up to 1000 characters, and is kept even if you later delete your account.**

## (d) Are Privacy Policy, Data Protection Disclosure and GDPR Policy updates owed in this PR?

**Recommendation (updated 2026-11-04): a short disclosure marked `[DRAFT — pending CLO/counsel review per #12011]`, written ahead of the processing, is added to the three documents in PR-B (Privacy Policy 4.17 and Section 7, GDPR Policy 3.19, Data Protection Disclosure 2.3(aj), 5.3(a), 10.3(l); mirrors and SHA pins in lockstep), because the CI DSAR lockstep gate fails any diff that touches the export allowlist without them. The full update remains a hard gate for the next step.** The earlier recommendation, below, was to make no change in PR-B; it is kept for the reasoning on drift risk, which still applies to anything beyond the short disclosure.

Reasoning. PR-B ships dark and the data subject is the founder-operator, a Jikigai principal, not a customer or a user of the hosted product in the sense those documents address. The published texts describe processing of users' data; no user's data is processed by this activity until a non-Jikigai founder is admitted. The PA-38 precedent shipped published wording ahead of the processing because that activity is customer-facing from the first enabling; this one is not. Amending three public documents, their mirrors and the SHA pins for a record that holds one person's rows adds risk of drift between the register and the published pages (the gates compare the two against each other, not against the truth) without informing any affected person.

Two points to settle first, because they could change the recommendation:

1. The Privacy Policy retention paragraph says account data is deleted on account deletion request. That sentence is true of the operator's account data but, read literally, is contradicted by the kept row. For the operator this is not a mislead of a third party; for any other founder it would be. Counsel can choose between leaving it with the operator-first rationale recorded here or adding a one-line qualifier now.
2. The operator account sits in the same user table as customer accounts. The record is the controller's evidence of its own principal's decision, which is why no customer data is involved; if counsel reads the operator as a user for these documents, a qualifier is owed now.

**Before any founder other than the Jikigai operator is admitted, all three documents are owed together in final form (replacing the short draft-marked disclosure)** (the GDPR Policy is the one most often missed): Privacy Policy (data collected, basis, kept after deletion, the note as a data category, rights), Data Protection Disclosure (processing activity entry and recipients), GDPR Policy (balancing test and processing register), each with its Eleventy mirror and the SHA pin refreshed, and the retention paragraph qualified.
