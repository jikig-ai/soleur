# DPIA Screening Addendum — Workspace Egress Audit Rollup (#9545)

**Question screened:** does persisting a 15-minute, per-workspace rollup of the
egress gateway's CONNECT decisions in the application database, retained 30
days and intended to become readable by every workspace member in PR2, change
the 2026-10-05 screening conclusion for the opt-in open-web-egress
sub-purpose, and what must happen before it ships and before it becomes
readable?

## 1. What changes in the facts of the 2026-10-05 memo

The 2026-10-05 memo screened a gateway whose decision logs are
"destination+workspace attribution only" and land on the existing journald,
Vector, Better Stack EU pipeline already recorded under PA-8. It did not
screen a second, application-side store. Upon merge of PR #10349, three facts
of that memo no longer hold:

| Fact in the 2026-10-05 memo | Position once PR #10349 merges |
|---|---|
| The only Jikigai-operated log path is the existing Better Stack pipeline (no new store). | A second store exists: the application database (Supabase prd) table `workspace_egress_audit_rollup`. The Better Stack raw copy continues in parallel. |
| Logs are an operator-side forensic record; no workspace member can read any of it. | The table is built so that members can read it. In PR1 no member policy exists and `SELECT` is revoked from `anon` and `authenticated` (nothing reads it). The member `SELECT` policy and read route land in PR2. This is the only change that fires trigger t6 literally. |
| Retention is the Better Stack envelope (90 days) and nothing else. | A second clock: 30 days, hard-deleted daily on `last_seen`, plus `ON DELETE CASCADE` from the workspace. The raw copy keeps its 90 days and keeps fields the rollup does not (see 2). |

The memo's own sentence "at most one WP248 criterion is partially engaged" is
therefore superseded as to the rollup (see 3). Counsel/operator: a
`Superseded` marker under that sentence in the 2026-10-05 memo, and a
frontmatter pointer key to this addendum, are owed (the append-only discipline
used for earlier addenda); this draft does not edit the memo.

## 2. The processing as designed

**Stored fields (nine):** `workspace_id`, `bucket_start` (floor of the event
time to 15 minutes), `requested_host`, `decision`, `source_host` (the
infrastructure host that emitted the event; not a person), `request_count`,
`bytes_out`, `first_seen`, `last_seen`.

**NOT stored in the rollup:** `resolved_ip`, `client`, `duration_ms`, and any
user, session, request-path or IP value. The gateway log line carries no
session or user identifier, and none is added. The Better Stack raw copy
continues to hold `resolved_ip`, `client` and `duration_ms` for 90 days; the
30-day figure must never be quoted as the total retention of gateway decision
data (see 8, inconsistency 4).

**Attribution:** `workspace_id` comes from the gateway's token-bound
authenticated username, not from anything the client supplies. Rows from
failed authentication (HTTP 407), the gateway's own `probe` username and any
non-UUID value are dropped before the database. (The plan records that the
"presented username is logged even when authentication fails" behaviour was
established by reading `egress-gateway-squid.conf` and the auth helper, not
yet by test against the pinned Squid image; the Phase 0 measurement closes
that. Until it does, the attribution-integrity statement here is a design
intent, not a verified control.)

**Write path:** Vector rollup, bearer-authenticated ingest route, one
`SECURITY DEFINER` RPC (service role only, no tenant `INSERT` policy), skipping
and counting rows for unknown workspaces and for workspaces whose
`web_egress` is false. **Collection kill switch:** a runtime flag defaulting
off, switchable without a deploy.

**Contract on counts:** the set of hosts contacted and the decision are exact;
`request_count` and `bytes_out` are best-effort lower bounds (restart-lossy
`GREATEST` upsert). An audit log must never overstate; it may understate.

## 3. Screening against Art. 35(3) and WP248 rev.01 (delta only)

| Criterion | 2026-10-05 finding | Finding now (rollup, design state) |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 35(3)(a) automated evaluation with legal or similar effects | Not met | Not met. The rollup scores no person and drives no decision. |
| Art. 35(3)(b) large-scale special categories | Not met as systematic | Not met as systematic. A requested host name can still reveal sensitive subject matter (a health, legal or political site). That is why Sentry payloads carry reason codes and counts only, never `requested_host`, and why the table is not exposed to members in PR1. Residual noted, not new in kind. |
| Art. 35(3)(c) systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area | Not met | Not met. |
| WP248 criterion 3: **systematic monitoring** | Not discussed beyond the public-area limb | **Now partly engaged.** A persisted, per-workspace, time-bucketed record of which external hosts the workspace's agents contacted, made readable to all members, is a record that observes and controls what a workspace's agents (and, through them, the people directing them) did. In a team workspace whose members are employees or contractors of the owner's organisation, it is functionally an activity log of staff-directed activity, so the "systematic monitoring of employees" concern is partly engaged. Mitigants: the subject of the record is an agent's connection, not a person's keystrokes; no user, session, path or IP field; aggregated to 15 minutes; 30-day horizon; no profiling or scoring; owners notified beforehand. |
| WP248 criterion 7: vulnerable data subjects or imbalance | Not met (population = opt-in owners and users) | **To be re-assessed at PR2, not concluded here.** Team-workspace members are not the opt-in party (the owner is), and an employee/employer imbalance is the classic WP248 example. The 2026-10-05 reasoning ("the feature is their opt-in") does not carry over to members who did not opt in. |
| WP248 criterion 8: innovative use of technology | Partially engaged | Unchanged, partially engaged. |
| WP248 criteria 6 (matching) and 9 (prevents exercise of rights) | Not met | Not met. The store is keyed by workspace and joined to nothing. An Art. 15/17 path must exist before external enablement (DSAR runbook is a PR1 acceptance criterion). |

**Count.** The 2026-10-05 memo had one partially-engaged criterion and
concluded the "two criteria in most cases" threshold was not met. With
criterion 3 now partly engaged, two criteria are partly engaged and criterion
7 is open. That reaches WP248's threshold in the partial sense. It does not by
itself mandate a DPIA (partial engagement is not full engagement, and WP248 says
"in most cases"), but the PR2 re-screen **must not default to "no full DPIA"**:
it has to reason from the readable-by-members state, and counsel should treat
a full DPIA as a live outcome. Counsel to confirm whether the supervisory
authority's Art. 35(4) list (CNIL for a French controller) names constant
monitoring of employee activity; this addendum has not verified the list
wording and does not rely on it.

## 4. Conclusion for PR1, and why it is provisional

On the design as it stands for PR1, **a full DPIA is not required, PROVISIONAL**:

- collection is OFF for every external workspace until PR2 ships (ruling A);
  the only carve-out is a Jikigai-owned canary workspace through a per-org flag
  override, which still needs explicit operator authorization;
- no one can read the table (RLS on, `SELECT` revoked, no member policy), so
  the criterion-3 concern is latent, not live, in PR1;
- the data is minimised to nine fields, aggregated, 30-day-bounded and
  cascade-deleted;
- the owner is informed at least 7 days before external enablement (ruling B).

The conclusion is **provisional** because it rests on four things that are
design statements today, not measurements: the 407 attribution behaviour, the
cardinality cap under a 100,000-unique-host flood, the measured rows per day
(the Better Stack pull of 2026-10-15 returned only the gateway's own probe
traffic from one workspace value, so real customer volume is unmeasured), and
the account-erasure path for solo workspaces (whether it deletes or anonymises
the workspace row). Each is an acceptance criterion or Phase 0 gate in the
plan. This addendum is not a statement that any of them has passed.

**The full t6 re-screen stays a PR2 merge gate.** It runs when the member
`SELECT` policy and read route are in the diff, against the readable state.

## 5. Lawful basis

**Art. 6(1)(f), legitimate interest**, for the rollup store. The migration is
to annotate the table (or each column) with exactly
`-- LAWFUL_BASIS: legitimate-interest (Art. 6(1)(f))`, and the Article 30 rows
cite the same basis. This is a deliberate departure from the Art. 6(1)(a)
(consent) basis recorded for the web-egress *capability* in the 2026-10-05
memo and in the existing PA-2 amendment: the owner's opt-in authorises agents
to reach the web, but it cannot be the basis for recording what other members'
agents did, so the audit record needs its own ground. Provisional three-part
test, to be confirmed by counsel and, before external enablement, written up as
a standalone LIA:

1. **Purpose.** Accountability for agent actions that leave the sandbox: let a
   workspace account for which external hosts its agents reached and whether
   each was allowed, and support abuse and incident investigation. Legitimate
   and specific.
2. **Necessity.** Host, decision and bucketed count are the minimum that
   answers "what did my agents reach?". The least intrusive alternatives were
   considered and rejected in the plan: reading Better Stack directly would put
   a workspace-unscopable credential on the app runtime, and a per-event spool
   would retain raw rows serving no stated user need. No user, session, path or
   IP field is needed, so none is stored.
3. **Balancing.** Expectations: the owner opted in to a feature whose upside
   includes this record, and is notified beforehand; members are not the
   opt-in party and are told through the owner notice and PR2 copy, with the
   record visible to current workspace members. Impact: low per record, but host
   names can reveal behaviour and, in a solo workspace, identify one person
   directly. Safeguards: aggregation, 30-day cap, cascade delete, no member
   read until PR2, no content, no per-user field. **Art. 21:** a right to object
   attaches to processing under Art. 6(1)(f); the DSAR runbook and the
   toggle-off path (stops new records) are the product routes, and counsel
   should confirm they suffice.

## 6. Roles (the plan's CLO assessment, recorded as drafted)

- **Solo workspace** (`workspace_id` equals the founder's user id): Jikigai is
  controller; the record is directly identifying.
- **Team workspace:** Jikigai is processor for the owner's organisation. This
  needs an Art. 28(3) instrument; the tenant DPA register recorded zero
  customer DPAs as of 2026-05-25 and the alpha-tester determination of
  2026-08-06 (#7331) applies the same processor/controller reasoning. Counsel
  to confirm the current state before external enablement.
- **Better Stack raw copy (90 days):** Jikigai is an independent controller
  (Better Stack s.r.o. is its processor under PA-8).

Open question for counsel (see 8, inconsistency 2): a processor acts on the
controller's instructions and has no lawful basis of its own. Jikigai chose the
fields, the retention and the purpose of the rollup, which is controller
conduct. The roles and the Art. 6(1)(f) basis above cannot both be right for
team workspaces as drafted.

## 7. Retention and erasure

30 days, hard-deleted daily on `last_seen` by a `pg_cron` job calling a
bounded batched-delete function, plus `ON DELETE CASCADE` from the workspace.
Toggle-off stops collection and purges nothing in PR1 (ruling C): existing
rows age out within 30 days or are removed on workspace deletion, and an
explicit erasure request goes through the DSAR runbook (export or delete by
`workspace_id`, service role). Ruling D records why: a purge-on-off would let
one toggle erase evidence of other members' agent activity. For a solo
workspace the Art. 17 cascade depends on the erasure saga deleting, not
anonymising, the workspace row; the work phase reads it first.

**Retention is bounded by an engineering ceiling and a legal number; the legal
number is the CLO's call and gates both enabling collection and merging the
PR1 legal commits.** This addendum records 30 days as the proposed number, not
as an approved one.

## 8. Items for the operator and counsel to confirm

Legal calls made in this draft:

1. Art. 6(1)(f) for the rollup (not consent), with Art. 21 handled by the
   toggle-off and DSAR routes.
2. WP248 criterion 3 treated as partly engaged and the "one criterion" sentence
   superseded; PR1 conclusion "no full DPIA" held provisional.
3. The PR2 re-screen must reason from the readable state and keep a full DPIA
   open.
4. No public claim is made; the owner notice and legal-document wording remain
   the last PR1 commits and a separate step.

Inconsistencies found in the plan and the existing record:

1. **Consent language in the public documents versus ruling D.** The privacy
   policy, DPD §2.3(ag) and the PA-2 amendment all say that turning the toggle
   off "withdraws consent". Under ruling D, toggle-off leaves up to 30 days of
   records in place. That is coherent only if the audit record rests on
   Art. 6(1)(f) (it does, here), but the public sentence ties the toggle to
   consent withdrawal without saying the record survives. The PR1 legal commits
   must reconcile this wording.
2. **Processor role versus own lawful basis** for team workspaces (see 6).
3. **Existing public sentences over-claim.** The privacy policy
   and the AUP both say "Every outbound connection is recorded in an egress
   audit log", and DPD §2.3(ag) calls the same records "gateway audit entries".
   The first two describe an audit log a workspace owner cannot see and that,
   until PR2, exists only in the operator's Better Stack store. These need
   temporal qualifiers; they are not edited here.
4. **Two retention periods can be confused.** 30 days in the app store versus
   90 days in the raw copy, and the raw copy keeps `resolved_ip`, `client` and
   `duration_ms`. Any public sentence saying "retained 30 days" must say it is
   the in-app rollup only.
5. **Article 30 and the memo list the log path as "no new recipient" and
   "existing PA-8 pipeline".** That is no longer the full picture once the
   app-database store exists; PA-2 (and PA-8's cross-reference) need a
   dated amendment in the PR1 legal commits.
6. **t6 wording.** The 2026-10-05 trigger fires "once the viewer lands", but
   PR1 already changes the facts by creating the store. t7 below closes that
   gap for PR1; the memo's t6 text is left as written.

## 9. Re-screening triggers

The triggers t1 through t6 of the 2026-10-05 memo stand unchanged. New:

- **(t7)** Re-run this screening before merge if **retention of the
  application-database rollup rises above 30 days**, or **any user, session,
  IP, client-address or request-path field is added** to the rollup (to the
  table, the ingest payload, the Vector rollup or the read route).
  Corollaries recorded with it: adding a member read path before the t6
  re-screen has run is a t6 trigger as well; widening collection to external
  workspaces before PR2's legal copy and the 7-day owner notice are live
  breaches rulings A and B and needs operator and CLO authorization.

**t6 status.** Open. The viewer (member `SELECT` policy plus read route plus
"View egress audit log" view) lands in PR2; the full re-screen against the
readable state is a **PR2 merge gate**, tracked on #9545.

## 10. Rulings carried (CTO agent, plan section "Decisions Ruled by the CTO Agent")

- **A.** Collection stays off for every external workspace until PR2 ships;
  Jikigai-owned canary only, through a per-org override, with explicit operator
  authorization.
- **B.** Owners who already have web access on receive a notice at least 7 days
  before external enablement (email plus a persistent in-app notice); no forced
  re-confirmation; they can opt out by switching the toggle off.
- **C.** Toggle-off stops collection and purges nothing in PR1; denied attempts
  made while off are not stored in the app database (they remain in the 90-day
  raw log); the RPC checks the toggle's current state, so buffered earlier
  events are dropped.
- **D.** "Off" does not mean "forget"; retention is already bounded to 30 days;
  PR2's toggle copy must say so. If the CLO objects before PR2 merges, D is
  reopened.

These are authorizations the operator still holds: enabling the flag in
production, creating the runtime flag, and publishing any notice or legal
text.
