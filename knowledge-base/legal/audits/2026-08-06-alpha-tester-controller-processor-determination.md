## 1. Verdict — DUAL FINDING. Disposition: **BLOCKED** pending §11.

**Limb (i) — PROCESSOR.** For the *instructed* limb of operator-assisted Soleur runs, Jikigai is an
Art. 28 **processor** and the alpha tester is the controller. This is not prospective. On
**2026-08-06** the operator ran Soleur agents on the **operator's own machine**, under a **JIKIGAI
Anthropic API key**, against the tester's repository content, **at the tester's request** (guided
onboarding). That is a verbatim instance of Posture B trigger 2 (§3). The Art. 28(3) instrument that
Art. 28 requires to exist **before** a processor begins processing did not exist. This is a
materialised lawfulness-and-documentation gap, not a contingency to be planned for.

**Limb (ii) — CONTROLLER.** For the *dogfooding* limb of that same run, and for the **ongoing**
observation of the tester's **private** repository via the operator's GitHub collaborator access —
under which the operator reads the repository's `knowledge-base/` git tree to measure knowledge-base
growth as a **Jikigai** product metric — Jikigai determines the purposes and means of the processing
and is therefore a **controller** under Art. 4(7). Art. 28(10) confirms the direction of travel: a
processor that determines the purposes of processing is considered a controller in respect of that
processing. The operator has elected to **retain** this access and paper it rather than revoke it,
which makes the controller finding a standing posture rather than a historical one.

**Disposition: BLOCKED.** Neither limb has its required instrument. No further Jikigai-keyed runs
against tester content until §11.C1 is satisfied.

## 2. The machine / key / purpose test, and the three postures

The determination reduces to a test a non-lawyer can apply without reading Art. 28: **whose machine,
whose API key, whose purpose?**

| Posture | Machine | API key | Purpose served | Jikigai's role | Instrument required |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Tester's | Tester's | Tester's | **Neither controller nor processor.** Jikigai is a software supplier; the tester processes his own data on his own kit. | **None.** |
| **B** | Jikigai's (or Jikigai account / Jikigai credential) | Jikigai's | Tester's | **PROCESSOR** (Art. 4(8)) | **Art. 28(3) contract, in place BEFORE the run.** |
| **C** | Either | Either | **Jikigai's own** | **CONTROLLER** (Art. 4(7); Art. 28(10)) | LIA + Art. 6 basis + Art. 14 notice. |

Posture A is the configuration the published documentation describes and is the configuration Soleur
is architected for. Posture B is entered the moment tester content reaches a Jikigai **machine**, a
Jikigai **credential**, or a Jikigai **account** — the credential limb is the one that fires without
anyone noticing, because no file moves. Posture C is entered the moment Jikigai reads tester content
for a purpose Jikigai chose, however small that purpose is.

The postures are not exclusive. §6 and §9 record that on 2026-08-06 B and C were occupied
simultaneously.

**(c) Therefore the published position is NOT falsified — it is INAPPLICABLE to the 2026-08-06

## 5. Art. 28 analysis

### 5.1 Art. 28(2) — sub-processor authorisation. **LIVE DEFECT.**

Art. 28(2) forbids a processor engaging another processor without prior specific or general written
authorisation of the controller. On 2026-08-06 Jikigai routed tester content to **Anthropic PBC** under
a Jikigai key. Anthropic is Jikigai's sub-processor for that flow. The tester gave **no** authorisation,
general or specific, and received **no** notice of the sub-processor's identity. This defect is live and
does not depend on any other finding in this document.

### 5.2 Art. 28(3) — the instrument itself. **ABSENT.**

The contract or other legal act binding the processor to the controller, covering the Art. 28(3)(a)–(h)
limbs, did not exist when the processing began. Art. 28(3) is not satisfied retroactively by a later
instrument; a later instrument governs **forward**. This is the central defect.

### 5.3 Art. 28(4) — downward flow-through. **INCHOATE, not breached.**

**A prior draft of this analysis asserted that `knowledge-base/legal/data-processing-agreements/anthropic.md`
"does not discharge Art. 28(4)." That claim was overruled and is not carried forward.** It conflated
Jikigai's own internal snapshot memo with **Anthropic's actual DPA**, which auto-incorporates via the
Anthropic Commercial Terms §C and carries Art. 28(3) terms, EU-US DPF, SCCs modules 2+3, the UK IDTA and
the Swiss Addendum. The downstream terms exist.

The correct analysis is that **Art. 28(4) is inchoate**: it obliges a processor to impose on its
sub-processor the same data-protection obligations *as are set out in the contract between controller
and processor*. There is no controller-processor contract with the tester yet, so there is nothing to
mirror downward. Art. 28(4) cannot be breached before Art. 28(3) exists — it becomes assessable the
moment the instrument in §11.C1 is signed, at which point the mirroring must be checked rather than
assumed.

Two caveats to carry:

- **Commercial vs Consumer Terms.** The auto-incorporation reasoning holds only if the Anthropic account
  used on 2026-08-06 is on **Commercial** Terms. Confirm the account tier; a Consumer-Terms account does
  not carry the DPA by that route.
- **The "fully liable" limb.** Art. 28(4)'s closing limb makes the initial processor **fully liable** to
  the controller for the sub-processor's performance of its obligations. That attaches to Jikigai
  regardless of how good Anthropic's DPA is, and is not discharged by pointing at it.

### 5.4 Retention window. **LIVE DEFECT.**

`knowledge-base/legal/data-processing-agreements/anthropic.md` records in its frontmatter
`zero_retention_amendment: unsigned`, and its Zero-Retention section states that Anthropic's default
Commercial Terms include a **30-day API request/response retention window** for safety review. Until
that amendment is signed, tester content sent under the Jikigai key attracts a 30-day retention at a
US sub-processor. Under Posture A this window attaches to the **tester's own** key and is the tester's
decision. Under Posture B on 2026-08-06 it attached to **Jikigai's** key, on **tester** content,
without the tester having been told.

### 5.5 The vendor memo's own re-evaluation trigger has fired.

`anthropic.md` records a "Re-evaluate when" trigger reading *"Soleur takes on data subjects beyond the
operator (cohort onboarding)."* Alpha onboarding **is** cohort onboarding. The trigger has **fired and
is unactioned**. The memo's `status_snapshot_date` of 2026-07-31 predates the event.

limb therefore cannot be dismissed as out of scope; at most it is low-volume.

## 10. Breach analysis — not notifiable, and that conclusion does not travel

**Finding: this is NOT a personal-data breach under Art. 4(12), and neither Art. 33 nor Art. 34 is
engaged.**

Art. 4(12) requires a **breach of security** leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss,
alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data. Here:

- The disclosure to the operator was **requested by the tester**. It was the point of the session.
- **No security control failed.** No access control was bypassed, no credential leaked, no boundary
  breached. The operator's collaborator access was granted, not taken.

A lawfulness-and-documentation failure — processing without the instrument Art. 28 requires — is a
different defect class. Art. 33 and Art. 34 do not reach it, and no notification clock is running.

**The counter-argument, recorded rather than dismissed.** Limb (ii) — the dogfooding purpose — is
**not** what the tester asked for. Onward use of a controller's data for the **processor's own** purpose
can be argued into "unauthorised" within the meaning of Art. 4(12), and that argument is not frivolous.
The view taken here is that it **fails on the security limb**: Art. 4(12) is written around a security
failure, and no security failure occurred. A purpose overreach by an authorised holder is an Art. 5(1)(b)
purpose-limitation and Art. 6 lawfulness problem, not a security-breach problem.

**Explicit limit on this conclusion.** This concludes only that **no regulator-notification clock is
running.** It does **not** conclude that nothing happened. What happened is recorded in §1, §5 and §6,
and is remediated through §11 — not through Art. 33.
