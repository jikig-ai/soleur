## 4. The DPD §11 replacement, read adversarially

I read the replacement against Arts. 77, 79(2) and 82, and against T&C §§15.1–15.3, rather than
against the register's summary of it.

**§11.1 — sound.** The DPD's introduction does state it *"supplements our Terms and Conditions"*
and *"is a disclosure document"*, so the referent verifies. Disclaiming a choice of law and forum
of its own is the correct fix: a disclosure with no acceptance record could not found an Art. 25
Brussels I recast jurisdiction agreement in any event, so the old clause was asserting a power the
instrument did not have. The closing sentence — that the GDPR applies as a matter of law and not by
contractual choice, and that no T&C term displaces it — is accurate and is the sentence that
prevents §11.1 from reading as a subordination of the GDPR to French law.

**§11.2 — accurate.** Art. 79(2) confers proceedings before the courts of the Member State where
the controller or processor has an establishment **or**, alternatively, where the data subject has
their habitual residence. The clause states both limbs correctly, does not misdescribe Art. 77, and
the closing *"irrespective of Terms and Conditions Section 15.2"* is the load-bearing sentence: it
stops §15.2's exclusive Paris forum from appearing to foreclose the statutory route.

**§11.3 — verified.** T&C §15.3 does say what §11.3 claims it says, including the home-forum
right.

**One drafting observation, non-blocking.** §11.1 cites *"Sections 15.1 to 15.3"* as stating both
the governing law and the forum. §15.3 is a preservation clause, not a source of law or forum. The
range is defensible as "the block that governs this subject" but a reader tracing the citation for
the operative forum rule lands on §15.2 only. Not worth a version bump to fix.

## 6. The non-favourable item, recorded expressly

**The DPD's removal of a home-forum promise is a narrowing, and this audit records it as one.**

DPD §11.2 previously read:

> Any disputes arising out of or in connection with this DPD shall be subject to the **exclusive
> jurisdiction of the courts of the Member State in which the User is established**, unless
> otherwise required by mandatory law.

That is gone. What replaces it preserves the **statutory** routes (Art. 77 complaint, Art. 79(2)
home forum for data subjects) and points at T&C §15.3 for consumers — but for everything else, T&C
§15.2's exclusive Paris forum now applies where the old clause promised the User's own courts.

**Who is worse off, precisely.** An EEA-established **non-consumer** User — a business Workspace
Owner acting as Controller — bringing a claim connected to the DPD that is not a data-subject claim
under Art. 79. They previously had a home forum by the DPD's own words; they now travel to Paris.
Data subjects and consumers are unaffected, and the fix removes a clause that was probably
ineffective anyway (a disclosure with no acceptance record cannot found an Art. 25 jurisdiction
agreement). But "probably ineffective" is not "no change": the clause was published, a reader could
have relied on it, and its removal is adverse to that reader.

**I record two further points rather than let them sit inside the resolution.**

- **The register overstates the outcome.** E2's resolution says *"the net effect on the reader is
  **more** protective than the clause it replaces."* That is true for data subjects and for
  consumers. It is **not** true for the EEA business User described above, for whom the effect is
  strictly less protective. The sentence should be qualified to name that population rather than
  generalised across "the reader".
- **The user-facing announcement does not mention it.** `TC_BUMP_METADATA.substantiveChange` says
  *"which courts hear disputes"*. A user re-accepting v2.5.0 who follows that to T&C §15 finds
  §15.2 unchanged, because the change happened in the DPD, which has no banner. The only narrowing
  in the change set is therefore the one limb with no user-facing notice attached to it. See §7
  observation 1.

**Disposition.** Not a merge blocker. The narrowing is narrow, the affected population does not
exist today (no arms-length Workspace Owner), and fragmenting data-protection disputes out of the
Paris forum would create the irreconcilable-judgments problem Brussels I recast exists to prevent —
the register's reasoning for not editing §15.2 is correct. Recorded here so that the change set is
not read as uniformly neutral-to-favourable, which without this section it would be.

## 7. The downstream Art. 13 question E7 creates

By stating in T&C §8.1c that Jikigai serves share links **as a controller**, this PR resolves a
real contradiction (a processor has no lawful basis of its own; PA-4 logs viewer IPs under Art.
6(1)(f), which only a controller can determine). It also assumes the duties that designation
carries, and the live one is **notice at the point of collection**.

**It is Article 13, not Article 14.** The viewer's IP address and request metadata are collected
**from the viewer directly**, which is Art. 13's trigger. The register writes "Art. 13/14"; the
Art. 14 limb belongs to a different population — the third parties whose personal data may sit
inside a shared *document* — and that duty falls on the **sharing user**, not on Jikigai, under AUP
§4.6(b). Separating the two matters, because they have different duty-holders and different cures.

**The gap, stated concretely.** An unauthenticated viewer opening a share link today:

- receives no Art. 13(1)(a) controller identity, no 13(1)(c) purpose and legal basis, no 13(2)(a)
  retention period, and no 13(2)(b) Art. 21 objection route;
- has **no route to any of it from the page they are on** — the page sets no cookies, runs no
  analytics, and carries only a signup CTA. Privacy Policy §4.9 describes the logging accurately,
  but the viewer has no link to it and no account relationship that would have delivered it;
- is not covered by an Art. 13(4) exemption, which applies only where the data subject *already
  has* the information.

**Why this PR is right not to fix it.** The cure is a product change — a footer link from the
shared-page template to a named Privacy Policy anchor, or a short in-page notice — not a drafting
change. Adding prose to a document the viewer cannot reach would not discharge Art. 13 and would
create the appearance of a cure. Deferring is correct; leaving it untracked would not be.

**Required follow-up.** Open a successor issue for the share-page Art. 13 notice, cross-referenced
from PA-4 and from the register's "What is NOT in this register" section, which already names it.
Recorded in this audit's `re_evaluation_triggers`.

### 8.1 Should the other notices' Last-Updated dates move?

**Yes for two, no for one, and blocked for three — and the blocking mechanism is itself worth
naming.** I measured each document's drift and each Last-Updated line's drift status before ruling.

| Document | Ruling | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Acceptable Use Policy** | **Move to August 11** | Drift 0 and its Last-Updated line does not drift, so the edit is mechanically free. §4.6 was materially restated. |

| **GDPR Policy** | **Cannot move in this PR** | See below. |
| **Privacy Policy** | **Cannot move in this PR** | See below. |

**Why the last three cannot move, and why that is a finding rather than an excuse.** Each of their
Last-Updated lines is itself a drift line, because the **mirror deliberately condenses the
correction history** and appends a note pointing at the canonical for the unabridged version. The
drift ratchet fails an in-place edit of an already-drifting line, so changing the date on both
surfaces reds the gate. The permitted routes are exactly three:

1. `SOLEUR_LEGAL_DRIFT_ACCEPT` — **excluded by the constraints on this review, and correctly so.**
2. De-condense the mirror's Last-Updated line so the pair becomes byte-identical. This is a drift
   **reduction** and would pass — but it dumps the full unabridged correction history onto the
   published page, undoing a deliberate readability decision. That is a design call, not a fix to
   be made under merge pressure.
3. Add a separate, byte-identical `**Amended:**` line below. Identical added lines create no drift
   and would pass, but a notice carrying both "Last Updated: August 9" and "Amended: August 11" is
   worse for a reader than either alone.

**The finding underneath.** The corpus has reached a state where **three published notices cannot
have their dates corrected without a break-glass**, because a gate is protecting a divergence that
was introduced on purpose. That is not what the ratchet was built to freeze. It should be resolved
by choosing route 2 deliberately — the mirror stops under-disclosing its own correction history,
and three Last-Updated lines leave the drift set permanently. **Successor issue**, not a merge
blocker: the substance of all three documents is accurate once B1 and B2 land, and a stale date
misleads nobody about what the documents say.

**Net for this PR:** move AUP and Disclaimer (free, correct, recommended); leave Cookie Policy;
defer DPD, GDPR Policy and Privacy Policy to the successor issue. None of these blocks merge.
