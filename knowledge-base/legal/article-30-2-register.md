## Why this is a separate file

`knowledge-base/legal/article-30-register.md` is titled *"Article 30(1) Register"* and is scoped throughout to Jikigai **in its capacity as data controller**. Art. 30(2) records a different thing — processing carried out **on behalf of** a controller — and its required limbs are different and fewer: the controller's identity, the categories of processing, third-country transfers, and a general description of technical and organisational measures. There are **no** purposes, no categories of data subjects, and no retention limb, because under Art. 30(2) those belong to the controller's record, not the processor's.

Keeping both in one file would mean one document meaning two things, with two different limb sets, under a title that names only one of them. That ambiguity is the defect #7100 punished in this corpus once already. Hence a separate file, cross-linked from the 30(1) register's §0.

**This register was created because processing was actually carried out** — not pre-emptively. A prior draft of the #7331 plan proposed omitting it on the ground that "a 30(2) record documents processing carried out, and none is." That premise was falsified when the operator confirmed the 2026-08-06 session ran under a Jikigai key: processing **was** carried out, so the record is owed.

**Art. 30(5) is not relied on.** The under-250-employee derogation is read narrowly here: it lapses where processing is "not occasional", and operator-assisted onboarding is explicitly designed to repeat across a target cohort of ten testers. The record costs almost nothing, so the derogation question need not be resolved in order to make the call.

### Status and the gap this record does not cure

**The Art. 28(3) instrument required *before* this processing did not exist when it occurred.** Art. 28(3) requires a written contract governing processor processing; the guided-onboarding session on 2026-08-06 ran without one. This register records the processing that happened. It does **not** cure the absence of the instrument, and must not be read as doing so.

The instrument now being put in place is effective **forward**. A recital acknowledges the 2026-08-06 session; ratification evidences good faith but does not retroactively supply a contract that did not exist. Anyone reading this record later should not infer from its existence that the timing obligation was met.

**Not a notifiable breach.** Art. 4(12) requires a breach of *security*; here the disclosure was requested by the controller and no security control failed. This is a lawfulness-and-documentation failure, which Art. 33 and Art. 34 do not reach. That conclusion is narrow: it means no regulator-notification clock is running, **not** that nothing happened. The purpose-overreach limb — the same run also served Jikigai's own dogfooding purpose, recorded as PA-34 in the 30(1) register — is an Art. 5(1)(b) purpose-limitation and Art. 6 lawfulness problem, and it is a certain one rather than an arguable one.
