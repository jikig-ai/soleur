# Alpha-Tester Processing Annex

**Between** Jikigai SARL, 25 rue de Ponthieu, 75008 Paris, France (RCS Paris 927 585 729) ("**Jikigai**") **and** the alpha tester identified in Schedule A ("**Customer**").

## Why this is a purpose-built annex and not the platform DPA

`knowledge-base/legal/data-processing-agreement-template.md` governs the **hosted Web Platform**: its §1.2 scopes Customer Data to that platform, its §5.1 defines Customer's instructions as actions taken in the platform UI, and its Schedule 4 describes seventeen technical measures covering Supabase row-level security, WORM audit storage and R2 object storage. **None of that infrastructure serves this relationship**, and importing it would assert Article 32 measures that do not exist here — the precise defect this repository publicly retracted in 2026-07 and must not repeat inside an executed instrument.

Its §7.1 and §7.3 also route data-subject requests to `/dashboard/settings/privacy`, an account page a CLI-only tester does not have. And its §12.1 liability cap, expressed as fees paid in the preceding twelve months, evaluates to **zero** for an unpaid alpha tester — a cap that is not merely unfavourable but meaningless.

So this annex is drafted for the actual relationship. Two clauses are adopted from the template because they are good and transferable: the data-subject-request SLA (§6.2 below) and the audit-rights structure (§9 below).

## Recitals

**(A)** Customer operates a private software repository. Customer is the **controller** of personal data contained in that repository.

**(B)** Jikigai supplies Soleur, a source-available agent toolkit. When Customer runs Soleur on Customer's own machine under Customer's own AI-provider API key, Jikigai processes nothing and is neither controller nor processor. **This annex does not apply to that use.**

**(C)** This annex applies **only** where Jikigai's founder ("the **Operator**") runs Soleur agents against Customer's repository content **on a Jikigai machine or under a Jikigai-held credential**, at Customer's request — for example during a guided onboarding or debugging session ("**Operator-Assisted Runs**"). In that configuration Jikigai processes Customer's personal data on Customer's behalf and is a **processor** within the meaning of Article 4(8) GDPR.

**(D) Acknowledgement of the session of 6 August 2026.** An Operator-Assisted Run took place on 6 August 2026, under a Jikigai-held Anthropic API key, before any written instrument was in place. Article 28(3) requires that contract to precede the processing. It did not. **This annex takes effect forward and does not purport to cure that.** It is recorded here rather than omitted because an instrument that quietly backdated itself would be worse than the gap it papers over. The parties' assessment is that the event is **not** a personal-data breach within Article 4(12) — the disclosure was requested by Customer and no security control failed — and that no supervisory-authority notification obligation arises.

## §1 Subject matter, duration and scope

**1.1** Subject matter: processing of Customer's repository content by Jikigai during Operator-Assisted Runs.

**1.2** Duration: from countersignature until the earlier of (a) the end of Customer's participation in the Soleur alpha programme, or (b) termination under §11.

**1.3** **Scope limit.** This annex governs Operator-Assisted Runs only. It does not govern, and does not imply any Jikigai role in respect of, Customer's own use of Soleur on Customer's own machine under Customer's own API key.

## §2 Nature and purpose

**2.1** Jikigai processes Customer's repository content solely to perform the software-engineering tasks Customer requests during an Operator-Assisted Run — reading, analysing, transforming and generating source code, configuration, schema, tests and documentation.

**2.2 Separate purpose, disclosed.** During the 6 August 2026 session Jikigai also observed Soleur's behaviour for its own product-improvement purposes. **For that purpose Jikigai acts as a controller, not as Customer's processor**, and this annex does not authorise it. It is disclosed here because Customer is entitled to know it occurred. It is assessed at `knowledge-base/legal/legitimate-interest-assessments/2026-08-06-alpha-tester-repo-observation-lia.md`.

## §3 Categories

**3.1 Data subjects:** natural persons whose personal data appears in Customer's repository content — including company officers named in business-register fixture data and authors recorded in git commit metadata.

**3.2 Personal data:** names, corporate roles, commit-author names, email addresses and timestamps, together with any other personal data Customer's repository content happens to contain.

**3.3 Special categories:** none intended. Customer shall not deliberately introduce Article 9 or Article 10 data into an Operator-Assisted Run without prior written agreement.

## §4 Jikigai's obligations (Article 28(3)(a)–(h))

Jikigai shall:

**(a)** process Customer's personal data **only on Customer's documented instructions**, including as to third-country transfers, unless required otherwise by Union or Member State law, in which case Jikigai shall inform Customer before processing unless that law prohibits it;

**(b)** ensure that the Operator is bound by an appropriate duty of confidentiality;

**(c)** implement the technical and organisational measures at §7;

**(d)** engage sub-processors only in accordance with §5;

**(e)** assist Customer, by appropriate measures, in responding to data-subject requests, on the terms at §6;

**(f)** assist Customer in complying with Articles 32 to 36, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to Jikigai;

**(g)** at Customer's election, delete or return all Customer personal data at the end of the provision of services, and delete existing copies, save where Union or Member State law requires storage — see §11.2 for the one qualification Jikigai cannot presently satisfy; and

**(h)** make available to Customer all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28, and allow for and contribute to audits, on the terms at §9.

**4.2** Jikigai shall immediately inform Customer if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes the GDPR or other Union or Member State data-protection law.

## §5 Sub-processors (Article 28(2) and (4))

**5.1 Authorised sub-processor.** Customer authorises **Anthropic PBC**, 548 Market Street, San Francisco, CA 94104, United States, for the purpose of large-language-model inference during Operator-Assisted Runs.

**5.2 Transfer mechanism.** Anthropic is certified under the **EU-US Data Privacy Framework**; Jikigai's agreement with Anthropic additionally incorporates **Standard Contractual Clauses Modules 2 and 3** and the **UK International Data Transfer Addendum**.

**5.3 Retention — disclosed because it is adverse.** Jikigai's Anthropic account does **not** currently carry a zero-retention amendment. Content transmitted to Anthropic under a Jikigai-held key is therefore subject to Anthropic's **default retention period of thirty (30) days**, during which **Jikigai cannot compel earlier deletion**. This directly limits what Jikigai can deliver on an erasure request (see §6.3). Jikigai shall notify Customer if this position changes.

**5.4 Customer's practical alternative.** Because this exposure arises solely from the use of a *Jikigai-held* key, Customer may eliminate it entirely by requiring that Operator-Assisted Runs use **Customer's own API key**. Jikigai will comply with such a requirement without charge, and in fact operates on that basis by default under §7.1.

**5.5 New sub-processors.** Jikigai shall give Customer at least **thirty (30) days'** written notice before authorising any further sub-processor. Customer may object on reasonable data-protection grounds; absent resolution, Customer may terminate under §11 without penalty.

**5.6 Liability for sub-processors.** Jikigai remains fully liable to Customer for the performance of any sub-processor's obligations.

## §6 Data-subject rights (Article 28(3)(e))

**6.1 Routing.** Requests are routed to **legal@jikigai.com**. This annex deliberately does **not** route requests through the Soleur Web Platform's self-serve privacy page: Customer is a command-line user and holds no platform account.

**6.2 Service levels.** *(adopted from the platform DPA §7.2, which is sound and transfers unchanged)* Jikigai shall acknowledge receipt within **2 business days** and provide a substantive response — data delivery, a reasoned refusal, or a status update for complex requests — within **10 business days** of acknowledgement. Article 12(3)'s one-month deadline applies as the outer envelope; the 10-business-day service level is a faster operator commitment.

**6.3 Erasure — a limit stated rather than glossed.** On Customer's instruction Jikigai shall delete Customer personal data from the Operator's working environment. Jikigai **cannot** accelerate deletion of content already transmitted to Anthropic within the thirty-day window at §5.3. Jikigai shall say so plainly in response to any erasure request affected by it, rather than reporting the request as fully satisfied.

**6.4 Costs.** Jikigai bears the reasonable costs of its assistance under this §6, except for requests that are manifestly unfounded or excessive within Article 12(5).

## §7 Technical and organisational measures (Article 32)

**These are the measures that actually apply to this relationship. They are deliberately few, and no measure is listed that does not operate here.**

**7.1 Credential control — the primary measure.** Jikigai's standing rule is that Operator-Assisted Runs use **Customer's machine and Customer's API key**. Where that is not possible, no run takes place until this annex is in force. This is the control that removes the exposure rather than mitigating it, and it is why it is listed first.

**7.2 Confinement.** Customer content processed during an Operator-Assisted Run resides only in the Operator's local working copy and in transit to the model provider. Jikigai operates **no server-side store** of Customer repository content. Nothing is written to Jikigai's hosted platform.

**7.3 Encryption.** TLS 1.2 or higher in transit to the model provider. Full-disk encryption on the Operator's workstation.

**7.4 Access.** The Operator is Jikigai's sole personnel. Access to any repository access grant Customer provides is limited to the Operator and is repository-scoped rather than organisation-wide.

**7.5 No republication.** Customer content shall not be published, quoted or reproduced in any Jikigai commit, issue, digest, case study or marketing material.

**7.6 Records.** Jikigai maintains an Article 30(2) record of this processing at `knowledge-base/legal/article-30-2-register.md`.

**7.7 What Jikigai does not have.** Jikigai holds **no SOC 2 report, no ISO 27001 certification, and no third-party security audit**, and this annex creates no commitment to obtain any. Jikigai is a one-person company. This is stated so Customer can weigh it, not buried.

## §8 Personal-data breach (Articles 33 and 34)

**8.1** Jikigai shall notify Customer **without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours** of becoming aware of a personal-data breach affecting Customer personal data, and in any event in time for Customer to meet its own 72-hour obligation under Article 33.

**8.2** The notification shall describe the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed.

**9.1** Customer may exercise an audit right once per calendar year on **30 days'** written notice to legal@jikigai.com, identifying the scope. Customer bears the costs, except where the audit identifies material non-compliance by Jikigai, in which case Jikigai bears its own costs and reasonable third-party auditor fees.

**9.2 Information in lieu of inspection.** Jikigai may satisfy §9.1 by providing its compliance-posture summary, the relevant determinations and audits under `knowledge-base/legal/audits/`, and the Article 30(2) record for this processing. Because Soleur is developed in the open, Customer can additionally inspect the source of any Soleur component processing its data.

**9.3** Nothing in §9.2 limits Customer's right to a genuine audit where material non-compliance is identified.
