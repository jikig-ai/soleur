## IndieHackers

**Title:** I couldn't tell my parked ideas from my forgotten ones, so I gave every idea one of four places

**Body:**

I run my company alone, with an AI team doing a lot of the work. My problem wasn't too few ideas. It was that I couldn't tell which ones I had decided to park and which ones I had simply forgotten. From the outside they look the same.

That cost me in two ways. I kept re-arguing decisions I'd already made, because I never wrote down why. And good ideas sat in my notes with no decision attached until the moment for them had passed.

The fix I use now is a rule, not a tool. Every idea goes in one of four places:

1. Doing now
2. Later, chosen on purpose
3. Not yet defined: I can't yet say what question it would answer
4. Decided against, with one line on why

Anything in none of the four wasn't parked. It was forgotten.

Two of those places, not yet defined and decided against, come from Matt Pocock's free, public collection of methods for working with AI (https://github.com/mattpocock/skills). I added them to the now and later lists I already had, and added the rule that anything in none of the four was forgotten.

My own AI team had blind spots too: the part of it that plans our roadmap once pointed me at the wrong priority, and it saw only about a quarter of our current plan. It never warned me about either. Now it reads all of it, and tells me when it can't.

The full post has the five-step sort I ran on my own list: https://soleur.ai/blog/parked-vs-forgotten-ideas/?utm_source=indiehackers&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=parked-vs-forgotten-ideas

## Reddit

**Subreddit:** r/solopreneur, r/startups, r/Entrepreneur
**Title:** How I stopped re-arguing decisions I'd already made: every idea gets one of four places

**Body:**

I kept running into the same problem running a company solo: a parked idea and a forgotten one look identical on a list. So I'd re-debate things I had settled months ago, or find an idea I liked and realize I never decided anything about it.

What fixed it was one rule. Every idea goes in exactly one of four places:

- **Doing now**
- **Later, chosen on purpose** (a decision, not a delay)
- **Not yet defined**: you can't yet say what question it would answer. "Do a podcast" has no question yet. "Should a podcast replace my newsletter?" does, so that one goes under Later.
- **Decided against, with one line on why.** If you change your mind, that's a new decision, not a reopened one.

Anything in none of the four wasn't parked. It was forgotten.

Two of the four, not yet defined and decided against, come from Matt Pocock's free, public collection of methods for working with AI.

The one-line reason is the part that saves the most arguments with yourself. When the idea comes back, you read the line before you debate it again.
