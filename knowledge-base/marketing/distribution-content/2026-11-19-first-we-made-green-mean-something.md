## LinkedIn Personal

A green check mark is the bargain every founder makes with AI tools: you stopped reading every line because something promised to check it. Our own scoreboard turned out to be lying — at least four different ways.

A check that reported "skipped" counted as a pass. A whole directory of checks was invisible to the finder whose only job is counting them. The map that picks which checks a change needs forgot a guard the moment its file was deleted — removing a protected file became the safest-looking green a diff could produce. And a baseline of excused failures meant "pass" had quietly become "nothing new is broken, we think."

Seven repairs to what a verdict means — six before the first speed change shipped. The flagship speed change was gated to land last of all, because speed on top of a dishonest verdict is a faster lie. With the scoreboard rebuilt, an opt-in side-by-side mode measured 1.88× faster on a small fixed set; the serial default is untouched. One repair still has a soak running — an honest "not yet" beats a convenient "sure."

The order is the whole lesson: ask what green means before you ask how fast it runs.
